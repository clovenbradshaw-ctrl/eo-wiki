## A vocabulary problem disguised as a philosophy problem

[Nine Instructions](/nine-instructions) argues that EO's contract system is an ordinary, well-proven effect system over one closed alphabet, and that the actually novel bet is which operations get promoted to first-class, contracted status. It names the promotion of NUL and SIG as the clearest evidence: "the derived void-null decides what counts as signal without a hand-set threshold." `native/docs/THE-NULL-STATES.md` is where that promotion is made good on in code, and this article verifies it line by line rather than takes the document's own summary of itself on faith — including the two places that summary turns out to be measurably wrong.

The document's premise is blunt: a positive finding is one shape, a null is many. `nul/index.js`'s own header states the discipline in one line — every entry in its gap registry is "the same act at a different grain: refusing a claim" (`native/legacy-ported/nul/index.js:36`). The [ways of knowing](/the-ways-of-knowing) register calls this spoke APOPHASIS — knowing-the-absence — and gives it one validity condition: the refusal has to have *reached* its object before it counts as knowledge rather than silence. A system that cannot fail this particular way cannot know anything by this route. What follows is a census of how much of that failure-vocabulary is actually built, checked against the code rather than the document's account of itself.

## The six laws

`THE-NULL-STATES.md:19–26` states six laws — the same count the wiki's own [Notes on NUL Refinement](/notes-on-nul-refinement) already gives in its resolution section — and they survive re-reading against the code:

1. **A null names its scope** — the extent, the pool, the cursor, the draws it was searched over. A gap without a denominator is, in the document's phrase, "a verdict wearing a count."
2. **A null names what would fill it** — its own cancellation trigger. A void re-zeros on one arrival; a cut is contested by a link; `unread_extent` fills as the cursor moves.
3. **A null is a finding only when the search reached its object.** "Nothing beat the ceiling" is a measured result; "the driver never ran the arm" is a fact about the reader, and the document insists the two must never share a word.
4. **A null never convicts.** An organ may say it has nothing to compare against; it may never manufacture a negative finding out of that nothing.
5. **Nulls compose downward.** A null at Ground makes every Figure and Pattern built over it a null too — presupposition, not falsity, citing Strawson and `verification.js`'s domain order.
6. **The mouth may relay a declared null and may never declare one.** The document's own example of the violation: "There is no mention of anything else" was the generation surface declaring an absence it never measured.

One citation embedded in that list does not survive its own scrutiny: `verification.js` is where the source document says the "Link and Lens cells typed GAPS, never falses" logic for law 5 lives, but no file by that name — and no other file returning that exact language — exists anywhere in this checkout. The downward-composition rule itself is not in doubt; the pairing rule and the grid both assume it, and nothing here contradicts it. But the specific file offered as its home does not resolve, and per the document's own law 3, that is worth saying plainly rather than repeating the citation as if it checked out.

Law 3 is the one this article can actually watch execute. `native/eval/the-fold/lib/product-assay.mjs:440–458` runs three declarations of the same void — "the Northgate Observatory closed" — against the same corpus: one with no scope at all (refused, `no_scope`), one after a single passage has been read (`reached: false`), and one after every passage has been read (`reached: true`, and only then does the document call it a finding). One arrival then re-zeros it, and `O.hl.voidTimeline` reads back the exact sequence `declared → filled`. The assertion the harness checks (`wall("9", "void-through-time", …)`, line 457) requires all of this to hold in one run, not as four separate claims — the no-scope refusal, the unread-is-not-a-finding distinction, the single re-zero, and the timeline, together, on live output. This is what "the search reached its object" costs to actually enforce rather than assert.

## Twenty-nine named nothings, not twenty-eight

The document's own standing note says the census is "read off the code — `nul/index.js::GAP_TYPES` (28)" (`THE-NULL-STATES.md:3`), and the wiki's own [Notes on NUL Refinement](/notes-on-nul-refinement) repeats the same figure. Counting the actual array in `native/legacy-ported/nul/index.js:39–68` gives 29:

`no_ground, kept_ground, unreceived_origin, degenerate_ground, undeclared, unknown_spec, empty_material, exceeds_witness, made_no_difference, unstable, incommensurate_extent, missing_kind_prior, slack_ground, anchor_ground, no_candidate, self_referential, paradigm_unraveled, empty_paradigm, no_rezero_trigger, not_earned, empty_field, byte_mismatch, overlapping_parts, gap_between_parts, undeclared_organ, undeclared_cell, payoff_not_confirmed, model_unreachable, trending_material`

That is a small discrepancy and not a damaging one — the document's own point is that the grid is bigger than this one array (SIG's `unheard`, CON's `unbound`, SYN's `withheld`/`vetoed`, EVA's `unexamined` all live in other modules and are counted separately in the grid at `THE-NULL-STATES.md:32–54`). But an instrument whose whole claim is that it counts precisely should not be off by one about its own registry, and the honest move — consistent with law 3 above — is to say so rather than repeat the inherited number.

A few of those 29 carry a measured number worth taking seriously rather than trusting as a label. `trending_material` is not a hypothetical: measured on `emergence/activation`'s `recalled` field over "the committed Frankenstein fixture," it re-zeros "exactly 122 frames apart," and 30 shuffled-order controls of the same material return "mean 6.00 ± 0.00, identical to the real reading" — a metronome set by the minimum ground size, not a perception of anything in the text (`native/legacy-ported/nul/index.js:68`). That is a *negative* finding stated as plainly as a positive one: the trend was never cleared out of the ground, it became the ground, and the gap type exists precisely so that result is nameable instead of silently reported as a discovery. `irreversibility/phase`, by contrast, is the positive case done the same way: licensed only after `scripts/turbulence-growth-rule.mjs` measured it "above 84/96" real DNS lines with "mean displacement +0.361," against `burstiness` (unstable on 91/96) and `permutationEntropy` (censored on 93/96) failing the same material outright (`native/legacy-ported/nul/index.js:245–256`). Three statistics were tried against real material; one was licensed, two were refused, and the refusals are on the record with the acceptance — not quietly dropped.

The grid's own live census, not a synthetic example, is in `native/eval/the-fold/results/null-census-RESULTS.md`: six fixed drafts read against a corpus built one sentence at a time, where a later-stated sentence carries `unheard` until its verb is finally read and becomes `bound` — never before. One draft (`Rowan Vale preceded Owen Blythe`) stays `unbound` at the reader for the entire read while its fact is separately *derived* onto the record from a different cursor — "two grounds, two clocks," in the report's own words. Run under 12 seeded shuffles of the same six drafts, "final census identical in every order" — the null types a sentence wears are a function of the cursor, not of the order the passages happened to arrive in.

## The two cells that learned to say no

`THE-NULL-STATES.md:60–62` names the two most interesting entries in the whole grid as the two *missing* ones: SEG·Figure had no cut, and DEF·Ground had no void as an object. Not oversights — the document calls them "the two places the instrument cannot yet say 'no' properly," and ties three live failures to exactly those gaps: a denial read as a link with a minus sign, a generation surface declaring an absence it never measured, and a witness's ∅ carrying no scope. An amendment the same day (2026-09-05) closed both, and the closure is real code, not aspiration.

**The cut** (SEG·Figure) is `CUT_PREFIX = "cut:"` in `native/kernel/notes.js:89`. A cut is a denial heard apart from the link it denies, sharing the link's two ends and never its own id (`foldCuts`, `notes.js:782–788`). It is disputable, never a premise: when a cut meets its link, the ledger lands a *contest* — `dispute()`, `notes.js:696–717`, typed `CON·Figure·CONTESTED` (line 711) and carrying the denying source and the exact decider span in that source's bytes (lines 713–714); a later `settleDispute()` (`notes.js:729–747`) lands the resolution as the same typed act. The whole sequence through time — link heard, cut heard, contest landed, contest settled or conceded — is read back by `negationTimeline(log, linkId)` at `notes.js:920–938`, one event per act, each with its own sequence number; a conceded cut still shows up in the timeline with its concession after it, never silently erased. The toll on a contest is measured, not free: [The Two Doors](/the-two-doors) documents the same corroboration engine finding 2 of 12 real notes cross-document-corroborated against a mechanical baseline of 0 and a fabricated-note control of 0/4 — a "contradicts" verdict here costs exactly as much evidence as a "states" does, which is what keeps a contest from being a costless veto.

**The void** (DEF·Ground) is `declareVoid` / `foldVoids` / `rezeroVoid` / `voidTimeline`, all in `native/kernel/notes.js:790–908`. A void names a scope (`scopeIsDeclared`, `notes.js:808`) and is refused without one (`no_scope`, line 812); it names what would fill it (its own `end1`/`label`/`end2`, matched by `matchesVoid`); `reached` is computed directly from the scope rather than assumed — `scope.total == null ? null : Number(scope.read ?? 0) >= Number(scope.total)` (line 820) — so an unfinished read cannot masquerade as a finding; and one matching arrival lands `REC·Ground` automatically at the door (`rezeroVoids`, line 852) or by name through `rezeroVoid` (line 876), never a silent close. `voidTimeline` (line 898) reads the whole life of one void back as `declared → filled` or `declared → conceded`, exactly the same shape `negationTimeline` gives the cut. Both null-less cells were filled with the same discipline: an event with a cursor, never a bare state.

## A naming collision inside the very fix

There is a wrinkle worth stating plainly, because it is a live instance of a failure mode this wiki has already named elsewhere. `native/kernel/notes.js` carries its own warning, dated 2026-09-16 and headed "NAGARJUNA'S NOTE": *this file's `declareVoid` is NOT `the-fold/void-shape.js`'s `declareVoid` — same name, different meaning* (`notes.js:16–27`). The ledger's `declareVoid` is the object this article has just described: an append-only entry, "nothing heard for `<end1 —label→ ?>`," tracked through time on the same record as everything else. `the-fold/void-shape.js`'s `declareVoid` is a different construction entirely — one slot as an extent with dimensions, checked for what a filler leaves dependently uncovered, built to fix a documented failure where "Hannibal Hamlin" and "Andrew Johnson" were each read as *the* complete answer to "who was Lincoln's vice president" (`void-shape.js:1–27`). No ledger, no witnesses, no timeline. Two real, tested, differently-shaped organs share one name, disclosed only in a comment at the top of one of them.

This is not a defect the null-states document itself confesses to, but it is exactly the pattern [Holons](/holons) already caught one layer up in operator naming, and [The Two Doors](/the-two-doors) caught again in a stale `canWitness` citation nobody had re-checked. A grammar built to name every way this instrument can fail to find something is itself vulnerable to the one failure mode that has nothing to do with search: a caller who imports the wrong `declareVoid` gets code that runs, returns a plausible-looking object, and means something else entirely. See [Handles: Naming as Governance](/handles-naming-as-governance) for the standing doctrine this instance falls under.

## The null to fear

`THE-NULL-STATES.md:68` names one entry in the grid as worse than the rest: EVA·Figure's "unreachable guard" — "a check nothing can trigger passes forever and reads as rigor." It is not a null about the material; it is a null about the instrument itself, findable only by asking whether the check was ever actually exercised, and the document states plainly that this is "the failure shape this project has caught eleven times." The canonical instance is a likelihood ratio of exactly 1.0: a gate meant to require two independent sources that in practice never fires, contributing zero evidential weight however confident the surrounding prose reads. [The Two Doors](/the-two-doors) measures this same number independently, for a different mechanism — the sibling engine's model-as-witness — and draws the same moral: "a likelihood ratio of 1.0 moves belief by exactly nothing, however confident the prose." That two unrelated audits of this codebase converged on the identical statistic for the identical failure shape is the strongest evidence in either document that the meta-null is a real, recurring hazard rather than a rhetorical flourish.

It is also where this article's own verification runs out. `THE-NULL-STATES.md:68` names `record-log.test.mjs`'s mutated-entry control and `resolution.test.mjs`'s "own first draft" as what checking the guard actually looks like. A repository-wide search for either filename, in this checkout, returns nothing — no `record-log.test.mjs`, no `resolution.test.mjs`, under any extension, anywhere in the tree. That does not mean the discipline the document describes isn't practiced somewhere in this codebase's history; it means the two citations offered as evidence of it do not currently resolve, which is worth disclosing exactly as readily as the discrepancies above, per the document's own law 3: a citation that cannot be reached is a fact about this pass, not a finding about whether the guard is checked elsewhere.

## What none of this claims

The document closes its own account with a limit it does not let itself past: "Coherence never establishes correspondence. A complete grid of nulls means the instrument can say 'no' at every grain; it does not mean any 'no' is true of the world" (`THE-NULL-STATES.md:70–72`). Twenty-nine named gap types, two null-less cells rebuilt as timelined objects, and a meta-null doctrine with a measured 1.0 likelihood ratio behind it are all, still, claims about what *this instrument* can and cannot say — never claims about what is actually the case outside it. The document is explicit that the one filler for that remaining gap is an oracle, and that it does not pretend to have one. A grammar this careful about its own limits is, by its own admission, still only a grammar.
