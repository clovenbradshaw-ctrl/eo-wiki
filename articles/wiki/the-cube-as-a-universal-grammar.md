*A claim this specific either survives contact with Universal Dependencies v2 or it does not. It does — 25 of 27 cells, not 27 — and the two empty cells are reported as a question, not sanded down.*

## The claim, stated where it was first measured

`native/kernel/cube.js` is a small file — the closed nine-operator algebra, three modes (Differentiate / Relate / Generate) crossed with three domains (Existence / Structure / Interpretation), three grains (Ground / Figure / Pattern) inside each cell. Nothing in its first ninety lines is about language. Then, at `cube.js:95-141`, under the header `THE CUBE AS A UNIVERSAL GRAMMAR (2026-09-17)`, the file states a much larger claim in a comment before it states it in code:

> The cube is not just an operator algebra — it GENERATES the grammatical category space every language's morphology projects onto.

The three domains become the three grammatical departments — Existence is the nominal system, Structure is the syntax, Interpretation is the verbal/functional system — and the three grains become clause positions: Ground the clause frame, Figure the argument, Pattern the agreement system. `CELL_OF_GRAMMAR` (`cube.js:124-133`) is the first table built on that claim, and its own provenance comment is careful about what kind of claim it is: the cells were "derived by consequence, not assertion — the map's cells are the ones the UD_Ancient_Greek-PROIEL and UD_Ancient_Greek-Perseus endings actually settle to when tallied" (`cube.js:116-123`). Person 2 lands at SIG·Figure, Person 1 at SIG·Ground, Person 3 at INS·Figure; Nominative at SEG·Figure, Accusative at CON·Figure, Genitive at CON·Pattern, Dative at CON·Ground. `grammarCell(feature, value)` (`cube.js:138-141`) is the lookup function — the "universal grammar's projection surface," in the file's own words.

That is eight dimensions, measured on one language family, consumed by one test. The interesting question a claim this size invites is whether it survives being pushed against everything a real cross-linguistic inventory contains, not just Greek's endings — and that push is what the rest of the kernel does.

## Completeness made falsifiable by construction

`native/kernel/universal-grammar.js` takes the eight-dimension Greek result and states it against Universal Dependencies v2 in full: every one of UD's 17 parts of speech (`UD_UPOS`, line 53), all 37 relations (`UD_DEPRELS`, line 57), and every value across 23 morphological feature dimensions (`UD_FEATURES`, lines 63-90 — PronType, Case, Number, VerbForm, Mood, Tense, Aspect, Voice, Evident, Polarity, Person, Clusivity, and eleven more) gets a cube address, or the taxonomy says so out loud. The mechanism that makes this checkable rather than asserted is two exported functions: `taxonomyGaps()` (line 272) walks the entire UD inventory and returns every UPOS, relation, or feature value the taxonomy fails to place — "an empty result is what 'taxonomically complete' means, as a fact," the module's own comment says (line 271) — and `cellCoverage()` (line 285) counts how many UD categories land in each of the 27 cells. Both functions exist specifically so the completeness claim cannot be waved at; it has to be run.

The run reports, per `CODING-LESSONS.md:1452-1483` (Lesson 49): **the gap list is empty, and 25 of 27 cells are reached.** Two cells take no grammatical category at all — `cellCoverage()` returns a count of zero for NUL·Figure and INS·Ground — and the lesson is explicit that this is "a question, not a defect." A taxonomy that filled all 27 cells on its first pass over a real inventory would be more suspicious, not less; the two deserts are load-bearing evidence that nothing was forced.

One near-desert is worth flagging by name because it corroborates an entirely separate dataset. `cellCoverage()`'s next-sparsest cells after those two empty ones sit at a count of exactly 1, and REC·Ground is one of them (tied with INS·Pattern) — its only occupant `Voice=Mid`, the middle voice. [Holons](/holons) reports the same address — REC ⤫ Ground, there called "Dialectics" — as "the deepest desert in the capacity ground," 15 verbs out of a 32,000-verb, 27-language lexical census, empty in 17 of 27 languages, glossed as "transform the conditions for transformation themselves." Two independent measurements, on two unrelated corpora (a verb census versus UD's grammatical inventory), converge on the same cell as an unusually hard place in the algebra to populate — a coincidence a hostile reader should be invited to try to explain away, because the more likely reading is that REC·Ground names something genuinely difficult for human language to lexicalize *or* grammaticalize, not an artifact of either dataset.

The honesty runs deeper than the gap count, though. Every placement in `universal-grammar.js` carries one of three disclosed evidence tiers, and the module refuses to let any of them impersonate a stronger one (lines 29-46):

- **measured** — inherited unchanged from `CELL_OF_GRAMMAR`, "never overridden here, even where it strains the department rule."
- **declared** — placed by an explicit rule (a dimension's grammatical department fixes its domain, its clause position fixes its grain, what it *does* to its bearer fixes the mode) and revisable by a stated falsifier: "the cross-lingual test... is what can falsify a declared placement."
- **form** — surface features (`Abbr`, `Typo`, `Foreign`, `NumForm` — the `FORM_FEATURES` set, line 94) that describe an inflected form's spelling rather than its meaning, kept losslessly in provenance and deliberately never given a meaning cell.

`addressOfFeature()` (line 240) returns the tier alongside the cell for every lookup, so a caller can never mistake a guess for a measurement. And the guesses are kept small on purpose — `DECLARED_VALUES` (line 136) is a short, named table of per-value refinements, each carrying its own one-line reason (Ergative case: "the distinguished agent, as Nom is"; Prospective aspect: "a coming event, as Fut is"), with a comment warning that "a refinement that no measurement supports is a guess." The discipline is the same one `the-two-doors` names for evidence generally — a source only counts as strong on a typed, checkable basis, never a flag that can be silently flipped.

The taxonomy also discloses its own near-miss. Lesson 49 records a test built to show one relation landing in the same cell across two languages — English *of* and the Latin genitive both at CON·Pattern — and reports that it "passed by coincidence": every English adposition takes its cell from its syntactic label `case`, so *in*, *to*, and *with* land at the same cell as *of* whether or not their meanings match the genitive. The test is now a named `todo`, not a quiet success. A taxonomy built by syntactic department rather than by cross-linguistic meaning can produce a true-looking result for the wrong reason, and the lesson says so before anyone else has to find it.

## The round trip, at treebank scale

A taxonomy is a lookup table; `native/kernel/eot-rich.js` is where it gets used on real sentences. Every sentence becomes a record with two layers (lines 6-27): a **surface** layer that keeps the exact bytes losslessly, and a **meaning** layer that holds content words as nodes, with function words absorbed as cube-addressed markers on the node they serve (`ABSORBED`, line 51: case, det, aux, cop, mark, cc, clf) and every relation between content nodes cube-addressed via `addressOfRelation`. The meaning layer deliberately carries no word order and no token index — `opaqueIds()` (line 84) assigns each node a hash-permuted identity specifically so nothing downstream can cheat by reading an id back into position.

The round trip is measured in three separately-scored levels (lines 28-43): L1 checks that the surface re-serializes byte for byte on entry; L2 rebuilds the full annotation — lemma, word class, features, head, relation, for every syntactic word — from the meaning layer alone, with absorbed markers re-realized as words (`annotationFromMeaning()`, line 168); L3 regenerates word order from the meaning layer plus the language's own *measured* ordering parameters, never a hand-set table (`measureOrder()`, line 192, and `linearize()`, line 220). Per Lesson 49, across seven treebanks in Arabic, Greek, Hebrew, Latin, Sanskrit and Naija — **7,744 sentences** — nothing goes unplaced on entry, the surface re-serializes exactly, the full annotation rebuilds from the meaning layer alone, and L3's regenerated order scores Kendall's tau (`kendallTau()`, line 247) between 0.39 and 0.78 depending on language. The same per-language parameters — learned from nothing but which side of its head each relation falls on, and how far — recover the textbook basic word orders without a table of languages anywhere in the code: Arabic VSO, Latin and Sanskrit SOV, Hebrew and Naija SVO.

The module's own comment names which level is expected to be lossy and why: L1 and L2 should be near-exact by construction, because absorbed markers keep their attachment; L3 is "the part expected to be lossy, and measured" (line 43) — word order is a real projection, not a bookkeeping exercise, and the honest number for it is a range in the 0.4-0.8 band, not 1.0.

`native/kernel/eot-enrich.js` (Lesson 54, `CODING-LESSONS.md:1618-1647`) adds three further fields to the same rich record without calling a model: REFERENT resolves which being a node names via the existing referent organ (proven on "Napoleon's army retreated" and "the army of Napoleon was destroyed" resolving to one referent despite opposite arc direction); EVIDENCE reads the standing (`corroborated`, `single-witness`) of a claim across arcs and documents, using the same structural floor `organs/asserted.js` already applies to verb claims; GROUND reads the extent a statement is true of off the arc's own cell (SEG·Ground for an oblique, never a direct object, "however place-like the word is"). None of these three is a category English states in its own grammar — evidentiality, in particular, is grammaticalized obligatorily in some languages and invisible in English — which is the same point the taxonomy makes structurally: the cube's inventory is the union of what languages mark, richer than any one language's surface, because English marking almost nothing at REC·Ground doesn't mean the record can't.

## Built without a model: a real parser, run on real prose

The round trip needs annotated sentences to run on. Where do English sentences with Universal Dependencies annotation come from, for a page the reader has never seen? `native/adapters/text/english-parser.js` answers that by training a parser from scratch — no LLM — on the English Web Treebank already on disk. `CODING-LESSONS.md:1579-1616` (Lesson 53) reports four small learned components (tokenizer, averaged-perceptron tagger, arc-eager dependency parser on a static oracle, relation labeller) trained deterministically in **76 seconds**, scoring, on a held-out tenth it never trained on: word class 95.2, head attachment 81.2, **head-and-relation (LAS) 77.0**, lemma 97.4, tokenizer F1 96.2 — against a naive baseline of attaching every word to its neighbor, which scores 9.0 and 29.0. The provenance discipline is explicit: "the model states the treebank, the file's content hash, and its genre, period and region" and marks the last three as *declared*, never measured — the same measured/declared distinction the taxonomy uses, applied to the parser itself.

`native/adapters/text/clause-tense.js` sits one layer above the parser and reads tense off its output — not off a hand-written word list, but off the same UD conventions the parser and the universal-grammar taxonomy already share (`Tense=Pqp`, "a past before a past," is `universal-grammar.js`'s own value for the had+participle construction, read back at `clause-tense.js:12-13,93-108`). It carries a second, independent witness architecture: `morph-cues.js`'s stored per-language convention is asked about the same finite token the parser read, and where the two disagree the parser's value stands while the disagreement is recorded as a typed `contested`, never silently overwritten (`tenseOfClause()`, lines 136-161) — the same corroborated/contested/independent-source discipline [The Two Doors](/the-two-doors) documents for the reader's evidence tiers generally, here applied inside a single grammatical judgment.

This parser was then run on real 19th-century novels, not held-out web text: **War and Peace** (Maude translation), 72,022 sentences and 673,229 words parsed in 70 seconds, and **Tom Sawyer**, 89,026 words in 9.5 seconds — every sentence entering the rich record with nothing unplaced, every offset reproducing the source bytes exactly. And the same lesson discloses the honest cost of the domain shift: **9.3-9.5% of the novels' word forms never occur in the parser's training data, against 4.1% on its own held-out web English** — the gap between a period-and-register match and a mismatch, measured, not closed. README.md's own Handle table names the whole chain under one entry (`README.md:359`): "Chomsky — Principles are universal, parameters are set from little input; a grammar learned from what was heard reads what it never heard," covering `universal-grammar.js`, `eot-rich.js`, `english-parser.js`, and `clause-tense.js` as one lineage. The Handle names the doctrine, not the other way around — the interesting fact is not that a module is called Chomsky, it's that four files independently arrived at the principles-vs-parameters distinction because that is the actual shape of the problem they solve.

## Where it holds, where it does not, without hedging either

The clearest test of whether a "universal grammar" claim is doing real work is what happens when you give it almost nothing to learn from. `CODING-LESSONS.md:1485-1527` (Lesson 51) runs exactly that test: the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, roughly 11,000 characters per language, translated into **roughly 490 languages**, the same meaning held constant across every typology represented on Earth. Chomsky's own frame for universal grammar is *principles invariant, parameters set from little input* — the cube supplies the principle; the UDHR supplies the little input.

**The principle transfers.** A word's cube cell, projected from pivot languages by co-occurrence across the aligned articles, is checked leave-one-out against each language's own treebank prior, with every cut measured against that language's own shuffled null (an unstratified null was rejected outright — it landed at exactly 1.0 in all thirteen languages tested, because a one-off word trivially matches any other one-off word, shuffled or not). Nine of eleven checkable languages clear their own 99th percentile — French at 67.6% against a 38.0% null, Finnish at 77.8% against 48.1%. Korean fails and Hebrew sits at the null, and the lesson names why without softening it: both fuse particles or prepositions onto their host words, invisible to a tokenizer that has not been taught to see them.

**Two parameters do not transfer, and both failures point the same way.** Adposition-finding by co-occurrence fails outright — function words appear in nearly every article, so they never co-occur distinctively, and what the method finds (0-3 candidates per language) is partly wrong. Head-direction-by-entropy-asymmetry on raw text fails worse, and the lesson does not bury this: validated first against the seven real treebanks, the method **ranks them backwards** (Spearman −0.68) and calls every one head-initial — Latin and Sanskrit included, languages that are canonically head-final. On the UDHR, unchecked, the same method would call Navajo, Quechua, Turkish, and Basque head-initial. It is reported as **refuted by its own validation, never read as evidence** — the validation step existed specifically to catch this, and it did its job.

The stated conclusion is the sharpest sentence in the lesson: "parameters are grammar, and string statistics over an unread text do not see grammar." The route that made the reader literate in Greek and Sanskrit — a treebank, a measured ending-prior, a reader that leaves gaps rather than guessing, competence checked out of sample against a shuffled null — is the only route that sets parameters correctly; a shortcut through raw-text statistics does not substitute for it, and the lesson demonstrates this by running the shortcut and watching it fail its own check. Lesson 52 (`CODING-LESSONS.md:1529-1577`) reruns the same UDHR material through a swarm reader and finds the identical fault line one level up: in Korean, Hebrew, and Arabic — the three fusional languages that failed or sat at the null in the projection test — a *received* verb-finding prior is the reader's only way to find a verb at all (turning it off drops all three to zero propositions found), confirming from an entirely different angle that these three languages need supplied linguistic knowledge, not statistics over an unread text.

## The honest ceiling, one layer further out

Lesson 78 (`CODING-LESSONS.md:1882-1940`) is not about the cube taxonomy directly, but it completes the same arc for the parser the taxonomy runs on, and belongs beside the UDHR result rather than left out of it. A session found the from-scratch English parser genuinely mis-parsing locative inversion and long periodic sentences — worse on 19th-century prose than on modern web text, exactly the domain-shift gap Lesson 53 had already measured (9.3-9.5% vs. 4.1%). It built gold data the careful way: double-annotated, adversarially adjudicated, checked against a structural validator and live UD documentation. Round one worked, twice, measurably — 0 of 10 real held-out failures fixed, then 5 of 10, then 6 of 10, with no regression elsewhere.

Round two tried to generalize *on purpose*: for each of the four remaining failures, it diagnosed the general pattern and built 3-5 new varied examples per pattern, physics-checked and double-annotated, then retrained. Measured honestly on the real held-out sentences: **1 of 4 patterns transferred. Three did not** — despite gold built specifically, carefully, and correctly for exactly those three. One of the three failures is not a data problem at all: its only correct UD analysis is non-projective, and the parser's own `train()` step filters non-projective trees out before training starts, so "no gold, however perfect, crosses that ceiling, because the architecture cannot represent the answer." The lesson's rule generalizes past this one parser: separate what is durably true about a failure — an architectural ceiling, a named recurring ambiguity class (English zero-inflection homographs: *shed, cast, spread, cut, set*) — from the specific gold built to patch one instance of it, and when a category (like a word whose part of speech genuinely depends on context) does not hold still, prefer a live, revisable read over baking one more hand-built answer into fixed weights.

Read together, Lessons 51 and 78 are the same finding at two grains: a fixed set of *rules or parameters, however carefully measured or built*, generalizes exactly as far as its measurement reaches and not one language, or one sentence pattern, further — and the taxonomy's discipline of disclosing that boundary rather than papering over it is the thing actually being demonstrated, twice, independently.

## What the grammar buys, restated as doctrine

The cube's operator algebra was already argued, in [Nine Instructions](/nine-instructions), to be a closed instruction set rather than a metaphysics — nine operations an application's logic lowers into and lifts out of. `universal-grammar.js` is what it looks like when that same instruction set is aimed at a target class with an actual completeness theorem available to test against: not "all of meaning," which is Schank's uncheckable claim, but the Universal Dependencies v2 inventory specifically, which is finite, published, and already the annotation standard every treebank in the codebase uses. `taxonomyGaps()` and `cellCoverage()` are the falsifiers nine instructions asked for in the abstract, built concretely for one target class — and they returned an *empty* gap list with two disclosed empty cells, a from-scratch parser at 77.0 LAS run on real Tolstoy, a 7,744-sentence multi-language round trip, and a ~490-language test that confirmed the principle and refuted a naive shortcut to the parameters, all in the same module family. That is a claim built the way Codd's was — bounded, checkable, and just as willing to report NUL·Figure empty as to report Person 3 landing at INS·Figure.
