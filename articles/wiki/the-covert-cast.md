*A persona's presence is felt only through the facts it produced — never through its name.*

## The company behind every turn

`native/the-fold/earned-cast.js` opens with a ten-name roster:

```
kelsen, ranke, freinacht, curtis, barker, oracle, lavar,
terry-gross, eastwood, kubrick
```

(`native/the-fold/earned-cast.js:11-22`). Every one of these is a candidate voice for the cue bundle assembled before the model ever speaks — the file's own header calls it "the-fold's practice module," vendored *pure*, with no import of the running app, so a test can drive it directly without standing up a server. In production it is not a rehearsal: `proxy-runner.mjs`'s `earnedCue()` calls `cueBundle({ act: classifySpeech(task), state, depth: 1 })` on the real turn (`proxy-runner.mjs:2188`), and the resulting `mouth` string — a plain-prose paragraph, never a transcript of who said what — is what actually reaches the model's context.

The header states the design principle before a single export: "the mouth never knows it is playing a role: nothing here emits a persona name, a role instruction, an apparatus noun, or a covert vocabulary word into the model-facing string" (`earned-cast.js:7-9`). Everything below is that sentence, mechanized.

## What the ten names actually are — and what isn't there yet

A wiki that reports its curation pass honestly owes the reader the same audit it ran on the [Handle table](/holons) elsewhere: not every colorful name backs a working part. Checked one by one against the rest of the tree, the roster splits into three real categories, not one:

**Built, wired, and separately Handle-named.** `kelsen` is `native/organs/reasoning-lint.js` — its own header declares `// Handle: Degrees Kelsen`, after Hans Kelsen's *Stufenbau*, the norm-hierarchy that ranks validity, regime, and precedence. `ranke` is `native/organs/ranke.js`, named (per its own header) "by the user's direction… after the historian whose *Quellenkritik* this organ mechanizes" — chasing a claim to its primary source rather than the page that merely cites one. Both are load-bearing across the proxy (`proxy-api.mjs`'s per-turn `kelsen` degree, `proxy-runner.mjs`'s `DEFAULT_KELSEN`), and both start `"cleared"` in the trust ladder below. Neither, however, has its own row in `README.md`'s canonical Handle table (`README.md:250-266`); Ranke surfaces only in passing, inside a *different* organ's row ("Alexander and Ranke still adjudicate the cells," `README.md:276`), and Kelsen does not appear in that file at all. The same registry-drift the module census caught for organ coverage generally (see [Holons](/holons)) recurs here at the level of a single cast list: a name can be real, wired, and load-bearing, and still be missing from the one table that calls itself canonical.

**Doctrine standing in for a person.** `oracle` names a rule, not a file — "the oracle is not a teacher," the first entry in `CONVERSATION_FLOW_RULES` (`earned-cast.js:298-302`), enforced by `NEUTRAL_CHARACTER`/`earnedCue`/`chatVoidCheck`/`surfVoidInfo` rather than by any `organs/oracle.js`. `lavar` names an entire subsystem documented at book length in `LAVAR.md` — a frontier-model grader and reviser, not a single organ — and it is the one cast member that starts at `"sampled"` rather than `"checked"` or `"cleared"` in the trust ladder, its own intermediate rung.

**Named, declared, and not yet built.** `freinacht`, `curtis`, and `barker` have no organ file of their own anywhere in this checkout — no `organs/freinacht.js`, no `curtis.js`, no `barker.js` — and no `// Handle:` line anywhere wires one to them. (The single other hit for "Freinacht" in this tree is a bibliography line in `native/docs/THE-MORAL-HELIX.md:733` citing the real-world theorist Hanzi Freinacht as a source for the moral-development material — a citation to the person the module is presumably named after, not a code reference, so it doesn't change the finding: the module itself is unbuilt.) The module says so itself, plainly, in the comment directly above the trust table: "Layer B organs (kelsen, ranke) are built and wired, so they start cleared; the proposed ones (curtis, barker) and the built-but-unwired door (freinacht) start checked and must earn clearance" (`earned-cast.js:119-122`). That is a disclosed gap, not a discovered one — the file names its own fiction before a reader has to go looking for it. `freinacht`'s output shape (quoting the person's own earlier claims, flagging what "is not established by what you were given," `earned-cast.js:389-393`) matches what `native/the-fold/speaker-model.js`'s durable theory-of-mind functions (`durableFacts`, `updateSpeakerModel`) already produce elsewhere in the reader — but `speaker-model.js` carries no `Freinacht` Handle of its own, so that correspondence is an inference from the shape of the facts, not a confirmed identity, and this article marks it as such rather than asserting a wiring that isn't there to find.

So "the earned cast" is, honestly, a company of three different kinds of member wearing one uniform: two citizens the README itself under-indexes, two whose personhood is a rule or a whole other document rather than a file, and three forward-declared seats with no occupant. The trust ladder (next section) treats all ten as if the distinction didn't matter, which is either a design bet that it doesn't, or a debt not yet called.

## The trigger ladder: what a turn's own shape earns

Nothing in this file is switched on by an instruction, a keyword flag, or the model's own say-so. `classifySpeech()` reads the raw turn text against five ordered patterns — escalation, frame-ask, map-ask, assertion, then a bare `?` — and returns exactly one speech act (`earned-cast.js:104-113`). The assertion pattern alone is worth pausing on: it is not just belief verbs ("I think," "I'm sure") but the personal predicates a theory of mind has to capture — "I'm allergic to peanuts," "my favorite color is teal," "I'm training for a half marathon" (`earned-cast.js:93-102`). The comment is explicit about the stakes: "a personal fact that is never classified as an assertion never reaches the durable model" — this is the extraction the cross-session speaker-model stands on, and a miss here is a silent, permanent one.

`eligibleAttentions({act, state, depth})` then maps each act onto a small, hand-declared set of names (`earned-cast.js:144-213`): an assertion pulls `freinacht`, `kelsen`, `ranke`; an escalation reorders the same three; a frame-ask pulls `kelsen` and, if the record has settled, `curtis`. Two attentions are unconditional riders on every one of the five acts — `terry-gross` fires on all of them (`earned-cast.js:181`) — and one, `trajectory`, fires only when the turn carries a `thing` under discussion *and* the person is asking what it is (`state.thing && state.askArc`, `earned-cast.js:201`). None of this is prompted; it is a switch statement over a classification the turn's own text produced.

## The trust ladder — and the rung it hasn't reached yet

`DEFAULT_TRUST` (`earned-cast.js:123-135`) assigns each name one of three standings — `cleared`, `sampled`, `checked` — and `eligibleAttentions`'s `push()` helper enforces the consequence mechanically: anything not cleared gets suffixed `:checked` in the eligible set rather than allowed to run bare (`earned-cast.js:146-147`). The doctrine this implements has a name elsewhere in the codebase, and it is worth reading in full because the file itself does not claim to be the whole of it: "Trust-earned — the autonomy spiral: a faculty runs solo only after *checked → sampled → cleared*, and clearance is earned by the revision rate holding across two consecutive texts, never one. A single clean text is a lucky text. Any regression returns the rung to checked" (`READING-EOREADER.md:378-381`; restated at `LAVAR.md`, section 6). `READING-EOREADER.md` cites this whole three-sense doctrine — trigger-earned, truth-earned, trust-earned — to a file called `the-earned-cast.md` (`READING-EOREADER.md:371`); no such file exists anywhere in this checkout, so that citation is reported here as unverified rather than silently repeated as fact.

`earned-cast.js` says outright that it is not that mechanism: "In the real system this is the autonomy spiral… Here it is a declared field on the state, so the tests can exercise the labeling" (`earned-cast.js:117-119`). What ships in this file is a static table standing in for a revision-rate computation that would have to watch a name's output across texts and move it up or down the ladder on its own; nothing in `earned-cast.js` reads or writes `state.trust` at runtime beyond the one lookup in `trustOf()`. The distinction matters for anyone reading the trust ladder as evidence of graduated autonomy: as implemented here, it is a *labeled* graduated autonomy, not yet a *measured* one.

## The directors' duel: economy against composition

Two of the ten names are not gated by trust at all in the usual sense — they are gated by which speech act the turn is, because the rule is about delivery, not standing. `eastwood` and `kubrick` "shoot the same scene two very different ways": Eastwood is economy, "the shortest true answer, no wasted frames"; Kubrick is composition, "the whole framed before the first sentence, every word earning its place" (`earned-cast.js:182-190`). The resolution is declared, not learned: a frame-ask or map-ask is Kubrick's alone; an escalation or a bare question is Eastwood's alone; an assertion fires *both*, "because a position deserves an answer that is BOTH lean and deliberate" (`earned-cast.js:189-194`). The facts each contributes are equally plain at the mouth — Eastwood's for an escalation reads "when challenged, answer in a few frames — name the ground the claim stands on, hold it, and stop. Do not relitigate" (`earned-cast.js:468`); Kubrick's for a frame-ask reads "the whole should be framed before the first sentence — lay it out exactly, each part in its place, nothing blurred" (`earned-cast.js:477`). Neither sentence contains the word "director," "shot," "Eastwood," or "Kubrick." The duel is real and the reader never learns it happened; only its outcome — the register of the answer — reaches them.

## Terry Gross and the flow rules she keeps

Of the ten, only Terry Gross is described as *remembering law rather than producing a fact about the record*. Her section is titled "THE ARCHON OF CONVERSATIONS," and its own text frames her domain as "how the flows go — the rules of the conversational journey, not any one answer" (`earned-cast.js:291-292`), and `CONVERSATION_FLOW_RULES` (`earned-cast.js:297-338`) is eight declared rules, each naming its own enforcing functions rather than asserting an intention with nothing behind it: "the oracle is not a teacher" enforced by `NEUTRAL_CHARACTER`/`earnedCue`; "a gap is a result" enforced by `surfVoidInfo`/`chatVoidCheck` (the doctrine that *I don't know*, *the material does not say*, and *I didn't look* must never render alike); and, load-bearing for this whole article, "the journey, covert" — "personas respond without the person being told a persona is speaking… never a role the machine declares" — enforced by exactly the three functions this article has been walking: `eligibleAttentions`, `assembleFacts`, `bannedHits` (`earned-cast.js:319-322`).

Her actual facts at the mouth are read off measured conversational texture, not scripted lines. The felt-shape check fires first on multi-turn boredom — measured by "document-ledger's own Fisher permutation test over the assistant's actual turns, not a heuristic about one text's sentence rhythm" (`earned-cast.js:439-444`, cross-referencing [Pathos and the Re-Ground](/pathos-and-the-re-ground)) — then on single-text flatline, then on strain at `"strict"`, in that declared priority order (`earned-cast.js:445-459`). A flatline produces "the exchange has gone flat — no turn has changed the conversation; draw the guest back out with a genuine question of your own rather than ending the thread"; a strict record produces "the record is contested — hold the claim to its ground and let the person carry it further; do not smooth the disagreement away." Both are, again, plain sentences with no persona attached.

## The trajectory lens: naming a thing by its arc, not by the name for reading arcs

This is the pitch's sharpest claim, and it survives contact with the source. `trajectoryOf(history)` reduces a thing's history to a bare sequence of `{op, grain}` pairs (`earned-cast.js:231-233`); `trajectoryShape()` walks that sequence and returns one of eight labels — `unborn`, `born`, `contested`, `established`, `rezeroed`, `circling`, `weighed`, `marked` — read off the *last* operator the thing underwent, with a repeat anywhere in the sequence overriding everything to `circling` (`earned-cast.js:240-258`). `trajectoryFacts()` then turns each shape into one plain sentence: `contested` becomes "it began, was checked against an account, and is now disputed — not settled"; `rezeroed` becomes "it began, then started over from a new ground" (`earned-cast.js:260-271`). `identifyByArc()` compresses the same shape further, into a noun phrase a person could actually use — "an unsettled claim," "a claim started over," "a claim that argues in a loop" (`earned-cast.js:273-286`) — and `assembleFacts()` closes every trajectory fact with an explicit handback: "that is the shape of [X] — say so if you see it that way, or correct me" (`earned-cast.js:420-423`).

Not one of those sentences contains "phasepost," "operator," "cube," "grain," "cell," or "helix" — the very vocabulary the mechanism is built on, and the vocabulary [Nine Instructions](/nine-instructions) and [Holons](/holons) use to describe this same machinery to a *reader of the wiki*. The person gets the arc's meaning without ever being told the arc is being read, let alone that it is being read off a nine-position chain (`PHASE_CHAIN`, `earned-cast.js:69-73`) or scored by grain. This is the load-bearing move the pitch names: *characterize a thing by its arc without ever naming the arc-reading.*

## The ban: three lists at one seam, and a name it shares with another firewall

The mechanism enforcing all of the above is a single function, `bannedHits(text)` (`earned-cast.js:505-518`), checked against three closed lists:

- `CAST` itself (`earned-cast.js:11-22`) — no cast member's own name may appear in the mouth string, checked with a case-sensitive word boundary.
- `APPARATUS_NOUNS` (`earned-cast.js:26-46`) — "prompt," "passage," "retrieval," "chunk," "citation," "this turn," and over a dozen more. This list is, term for term, the same closed class as `APPARATUS_TERMS` in `native/organs/firewall.js:60-80` — the wall documented in [The Model Is the Leaf](/the-model-is-the-leaf), built after five live runs against gemma2:2b produced "The prompt specifically identifies Hannibal Hamlin as Lincoln's vice president" because the system prompt itself said "the prompt" to the model twice.
- `COVERT_TERMS` (`earned-cast.js:48-67`) — and this is where earned-cast.js goes further than `firewall.js` ever does: `"phasepost"`/`"phaseposts"`, `"trajectory"`/`"trajectories"`/`"trajector"`, `"phase"`, `"cube"`, `"operator"`, `"grain"`, `"cell"`, `"canonical order"`, `"helix"`, `"act of transformation"`. The comment names the reason directly: "words that name the identification machinery a person must never be told is running… Banning the words is what forces the plain narration" (`earned-cast.js:48-52`).

The distinction matters and should not be collapsed. `firewall.js`'s wall is about the RAG apparatus — it stops a model from narrating its own retrieval pipeline back to a person, and it is entirely orthogonal to EO's theory of operators. `earned-cast.js`'s third list bans EO's *own vocabulary* — the words this very wiki uses on every page to describe the mechanism — from ever reaching the person the mechanism is reading. Read together with the ten cast names, `enforceBan` (`earned-cast.js:520`, `export const enforceBan = (bundle) => bannedHits(bundle.mouth)`) is checking for three unrelated kinds of leak in one pass: *who* is speaking, *what apparatus* produced the material, and *what theory* the speaking is built on. All three are equally forbidden from the model-facing string, on the same footing.

One cost of that breadth, inferred from the code rather than measured against real traffic: several `COVERT_TERMS` entries are ordinary English words in other senses — `"cell"`, `"phase"`, `"helix"` among them — and `bannedHits` matches on a bare word-boundary regex with no sense disambiguation (`earned-cast.js:514-516`). A mouth sentence that used "phase" or "cell" in an entirely mundane sense (a phase of a project, a jail cell) would trip the same ban as a leak of the actual operator vocabulary. That is very likely the correct trade for a fail-closed system — the next section shows what happens to a tripped bundle — but it is a real, uncharacterized false-positive surface, not a hypothetical one, and this article is not aware of any measurement of how often it fires on legitimate prose.

## Fail closed: a leaking cue is dropped, never shipped

The ban is not merely available to be checked; it is checked on the one call site that matters. `earnedCue()` in `proxy-runner.mjs` builds the bundle, then: `const leaks = bannedHits(mouth); if (leaks.length) return null; // a leaking cue is dropped, never shipped` (`proxy-runner.mjs:2191-2192`). There is no partial redaction, no attempt to strip just the offending word and ship the rest — the entire cue for that turn is discarded on any hit. The whole function is additionally wrapped so that a thrown error anywhere in cue assembly also degrades to `null` rather than an exception reaching the turn: "the cue must never break a turn" (`proxy-runner.mjs:2194-2196`). Both failure paths land on the same value, which is the same as having no cue at all for that turn — the conversation proceeds without the earned cast's help rather than risk it speaking its own name.

## What this article did not find

In the interest of matching the evidence claimed to the evidence available: this pass found no dedicated test file for `earned-cast.js` as a whole. `native/tests/earned-cast-ground.test.js` exists and passes, but by its own header it "covers the one new seam this pass adds" — `state.groundFact` reaching the `"ground"` attention and the mouth — not the trigger ladder, the trust ladder, the director duel, or the trajectory lens documented above. `native/tests/ground-attention.test.js` and `native/tests/trajectory-boredom.test.js` each cover one further seam. No test in the tree exercises `classifySpeech`'s five-pattern ladder directly, and no measured false-positive or false-negative rate for `bannedHits` against real assembled mouths — as opposed to the hand-written fixtures in the three test files above — was found anywhere in this repository.
