*A reading can be wrong in a way nothing above the log can see. LaVar exists because the record has no oracle and needs one, at least at the start.*

## The specimen the whole charter is built to catch

A fresh constitutional read of *Crime and Punishment* produced `ref:auto:marmeladov` — one referent holding a father and his daughter under a single node: «Sofya Semyonovna Marmeladov | Sofya Semyonovna | Marmeladov | Semyonovna | Sofya». The fold was licensed by the containment branch of `namesCorefer` in `native/adapters/text/surfaces.js` (lines 189–196): a bare surname is a single-token subset of the daughter's full three-token name, and the file's own header treats containment as "real structural evidence" that fires with no witness whenever the shorter form sits as a contiguous run inside the longer one — an engine-tier rule deciding a question that needed a per-text coref prior (`LAVAR.md` §13). Tellingly, the file's own comments record that the *sibling* bug — two people sharing only a final token, with no containment — was already closed in a later pass by requiring an injected witness, cited against an almost-identical Crime and Punishment collision (Katerina Ivanovna and Alyona Ivanovna, two unrelated women sharing only a patronymic). Containment itself was deliberately left open, on the reasoning that one string actually containing every token of another is real structural evidence; Marmeladov is exactly the case that reasoning misses. Zero merges were ever recorded on the whole book; the node was admitted that way in a single act, so `reviewMerges` and `refuteIdentity` — the mechanisms that exist to catch a bad merge — can never reach it, because nothing was ever merged.

Every downstream property of the holograph held perfectly while this was true. Addresses resolved, compression compressed, two independent render paths agreed — because they are two walks over one return value and cannot disagree. What they agreed on was that a young woman and her father were one person. This is the case for [Frege's referent](/freges-referent-alias-equivalence-class) made operational and adversarial: the system's compression exists precisely to fold aliases onto one referent, and the same machinery that makes «Marmeladov» collapse correctly onto one man will collapse two different people onto one referent given the wrong trigger, with no error anywhere in the log to show for it. And the stakes are rising, not falling: a same-session retrieval change (`#164`) took anchor resolution from 34% to 44%, meaning *more* questions now resolve confidently onto readings of exactly this kind. Better retrieval onto a wrong reading is worse than poor retrieval onto it.

## What LaVar is, and pointedly is not

LaVar is a frontier model running in Claude Code, reading source material directly and inspecting what the small reader produced from it. It does not write the reading. It reads the material, reads the sidecar, and records where they disagree (`LAVAR.md` §2). The asymmetry is deliberate: the small reader is fast, cheap, structural, and blind in specific, nameable ways; LaVar is a literate-adult reader that is far too expensive to be *the* reader. So LaVar is not a better reader — it is a grader, a reviser, and eventually a spot-checker, and the design goal is for it to be needed less over time. This is the same shape of discipline the wiki names elsewhere as [witnesses, not verdicts](/the-guardrail-organs-witnesses-not-verdicts): LaVar's opinion determines individual revisions, but the *rung* — the reader's actual grade level — is a measured consequence of revision rate, never a judgment LaVar renders directly (§5).

LaVar produces exactly three artifacts (§3), and the discipline governing each is load-bearing:

- **Revisions** — a superseding entry appended to the same reading log via the ledger's own `supersedes` field (implemented in `native/the-fold/document-ledger.js` and its sibling organs, not one dedicated store file). The original entry is never overwritten; the fold recomputes clean. This is the charter's own standing discipline (`LAVAR.md` §3, §13): *"we don't need a system that's always right, but we do need one that is actively learning to get better."*
- **Live priors** — composition affordances and relational structure derived from readings LaVar has cleared, entering the hyperlexicon with LaVar named as giver.
- **A mistake corpus** — every revision, queryable as its own object, because a reading with mistakes on file whose fold is nonetheless correct teaches more than a clean reading does (§10).

## A ladder LaVar did not invent

Difficulty is a received prior, not a computed metric. LaVar's ladder is McGuffey's Eclectic Readers — seven public-domain rungs (Primer, then First through Sixth), graded by a named 19th-century editor for teaching reading by progressive difficulty in real literature — followed by public-domain children's and simple narrative material (Aesop, Grimm, Andersen, Potter, Alice), then short stories, then the novels the corpus already holds, where the Marmeladov specimen lives (§4). The charter is explicit that this ordering is a hypothesis, not a proof: §11 states plainly that if revision rate does not fall monotonically across rungs, "the ordering is wrong and the level column means nothing," and that this is checkable in the first full pass and should be checked before anything is built on top of it.

## The dashboard: two measured columns, because they diagnose different failures

One file, `native/eval/lavar/dashboard.md`, rewritten each run (§5). **Retrieval** is already wired: `buildFactBank` in `native/eval/the-fold/lib/long-stream.mjs` produces facts, and `holograph-reading.mjs` scores whether a question reaches the right passage, checked against a redealt-address control built into that same script's own CONTROL arm — measured on Borodino at ADDRESS 5/23, against a redealt band of [0,2] over 199 draws, p = 0.0050. **Revision rate** — revisions per thousand admitted notes, by rung — is the column that actually governs autonomy: *the reader's level is the highest rung where revision rate stays under a gate declared before the run.* A **comprehension** column (protagonist-centered narrative cloze) is named as needed but not yet built, and its absence is disclosed rather than papered over.

The autonomy spiral itself has three states per rung (§6): **Checked** (LaVar reads every passage against every sidecar — expensive, and where every rung starts), **Sampled** (LaVar reads a declared fraction, drawn by seed rather than by LaVar's own sense of which passages look risky — sampling by suspicion would bias the estimate toward what LaVar already knows to look for), and **Cleared** (the reader admits alone; LaVar audits at long intervals, and any regression returns the rung to Checked). Advancement requires the revision rate to hold under the gate across *two consecutive texts*, because a single clean text is a lucky text — and a rung the reader passes does not unlock the next rung until the next rung has itself been read at Checked at least once, since difficulty is a hypothesis about ordering, not a proof of it.

## Golden-first: a number instead of a feeling

The governing discipline of §7 is procedural, not aspirational: *build the golden reference before running the reader, or the exercise is calibration against the answer key.* `eval/lavar/golden-tool.mjs` refuses to accept a golden that fails either of two properties — every anchor must resolve, and every sentence must be accounted for, either as a proposition or as an explicit `EMPTY` claim naming why nothing was drawn there. Silence is forbidden because an omitted sentence cannot be told apart from a missed one; the first golden written for chapter 3 held 47 propositions, its author believed it complete, and the coverage check caught that it was missing the chapter's entire ending — a check re-reading would not have caught.

Read against 801 hand-authored clause-level propositions over *Alice* chapters 1–3, the reader recalls **11.4%**, emitting 454 arrangements against the 91 a reader would actually draw — it is not under-producing, it is producing mostly the wrong things, and 8.2% of all propositions are intransitive and structurally unadmittable under the present extraction gate. Every later claim of improvement in the charter is stated against this reference number or it does not count as a claim at all.

## S101–S105: a chain of confirmations that include their own negative results

This is the section of `native/READING-SPEC.md` where the wiki's own discipline — disclose a limit as readily as a win — is enforced by the record itself, not by editorial choice.

**S101, the floor.** 100% of a chapter's own words must be reconstructable from nothing but its ledger's `sentence`/`scene-break` addresses — checked with `eval/lavar/recoverability.mjs`. *Alice*, all 12 chapters, 26,171 words: 100%, zero gaps. A byte range nothing on the ledger addresses is gone for good — no rereading recovers it, because it was never heard.

**Confirmed, with real diminishing returns.** Rereading with the deepest available prior roughly doubles recall on a thin-prior chapter (ch1: 12.8%→22.3%; ch2: 14.2%→21.2%) and does almost nothing on a chapter that already had a decent prior (ch4: 12.4%→12.9%) — a measured curve, not a guess. A local model used as a witness on the ledger's own disclosed gaps is checked, not trusted: half of gemma2:2b's raw picks turned out to be pure position bias, caught only by asking twice with the candidate list reversed (`eval/lavar/witness-referent.mjs`). And `drill.mjs` shows *where* a reading is thin, not just that it is: *Alice* has 152 book-wide arrangements and zero with a resolved being on the other end; Dinah — talked about constantly, on stage never — has zero.

**S102, a second book, a real bug, a null result.** Spiralling to *The Picture of Dorian Gray* surfaced a chapter-heading regex bug AIW's own convention (every chapter titled) never exercised — fixed identically across three files, and AIW's own 12-chapter numbers did not move by one byte after the fix. Feeding AIW's earned vocabulary (287 verbs) into a cold read of Dorian Gray ch1 raised propositions found 32% (285→377) while leaving cast discovery completely unchanged (10 entities, 134 voids) — verified by grepping the new book's cast against every AIW referent and finding zero matches outside the lexicon's own disclosed citation.

**S103, the honest failure.** Five real Wikipedia articles across five languages, with only the POS prior and pronoun list swapped, held 100% recoverability everywhere — the address layer is language-general — but Korean and Hebrew's "propositions" were, on hand inspection, 100% built from embedded English citation and image-caption debris, never a word of the article's actual content, because capitalisation-based referent discovery has nothing else to seize on in a script with no case distinction. The charter names this precisely: *a confidently wrong reading, not an honestly empty one — worse than the silence a recall number alone would report identically for both.*

**S104, the pass that did nothing, and said so.** the-fold's `relative.js` Field genuinely recovers gold-verified content for under-covered referents at 73.5% in its own top-10%, against a matched random-cue control of 14.6% — a real, standing signal. Turned into a bulk pass (`eval/lavar/field-lens-boost.mjs`), it produced 68 lines that *looked* like 33 newly-recovered beings. Checked line for line against the golden, the actual number was different: **68 of 68 were byte-for-byte duplicates already in the ledger, at the identical address, with the referent still unresolved in both copies** — a pure function rerun on inputs it had already seen, returning the same output it always would. The underlying weak-referent diagnosis stayed real; the fix built on top of it did not close the gap.

**S105, the fix that actually landed.** `endRef` gained a third resolution tier — a substring-containment check that unwraps a captured phrase like "our Dinah here" against the same cast surfaces tier 1 already trusts, committed only when exactly one referent matches. Wiring it in surfaced two more bugs (a reread's staleness check comparing too narrow a key; duplicate contest lines on a rereread chapter), both fixed. Measured, whole book: 268 real, referent-scoped `EOTRevision@1` bindings appended across 12 chapters, zero duplicate propositions, zero duplicate contests, 100% recoverability held, and all four golden scores byte-identical to baseline — a referent-binding pass was never going to move a clause-segmentation metric, and it didn't.

## What LaVar checks first, cheapest checks first

Section 9 orders the check list explicitly by cost. **Self-contradiction** needs no model at all — an irreflexive relation whose two ends resolve to one referent is a single equality check, and it fires three times on the Marmeladov passage alone, including on bare «Marmeladov», the very token that caused the fold. **Wrong fold** — one person given two names, or two people folded into one — is where LaVar actually reads the passage; the negative control is Pyotr Petrovitch and Luzhin, who *are* one man and must stay folded, so the check has to discriminate, not just flag proper nouns. **Missed relation**: the reader produced 497 edges over the specimen passage and zero binding the two people it names, because the construction has no verb slot. **Missed being**: `extractSurfaces` finds candidates by capitalisation alone and returns 401 surfaces on the printed novel against zero on the same novel lowercased; `heard-surfaces` raises that floor to a disclosed 5-of-20 cast recall against the read path's 20-of-20 — and these misses are named as the most informative rows in the mistake corpus, because they are the ones a bigger prior would actually fix.

## The mistake corpus is a curriculum, not a log

Each revision records the rung, the organ that produced the original, the class from the check list above, and the material's own address (§10). The question the corpus answers is not *how many* mistakes but *which organ fails at which rung* — because that names the next thing to build, not the next thing to tune. A reading whose fold is correct and whose ledger holds three superseded entries is worth more than a clean one, and the record is built to make that legible rather than hiding the history behind the fold.

## Looking: when the failure is one register down from the reading

*"LaVar should tell us if we are reading well and should be adapted as needed to help trigger 'looking.'"* (§14, user standing, verbatim.) The Marmeladov fold was a perfectly faithful projection of a wrong reading; "looking" names the same class of failure one level down, at sense rather than semantics — a table, a box-drawn diagram, or sub-sentence lines that are cells rather than sentences get read wrong *before* a single referent resolves, because the plain-text reader cannot structurally see formatting at all.

`native/organs/look.js` gives this a mechanism with two judged senses, mirroring the corroboration discipline used everywhere else in the reader: a mechanical pass (OpenCV box/connector detection plus per-region Tesseract OCR) and a vision pass (a local vision model's holistic read, escalated only on a real, named disagreement with the mechanical facts). `weirdFormattingScore` names *when* the reader is misreading formatting — table rows, box-drawing, very short lines, wide whitespace runs — `renderTextToImage` renders those bytes the way a person would see them, and `lookAtImage` reads the render, admitted into the ledger as its own sourced observation (`<rel>::look`). `lavarGradeReading` in `native/the-fold/document-ledger.js` returns, per source, whether the reader read those bytes well or should look instead — a `shouldLook` verdict is what fires the looking pass, not a hand-set threshold.

## Closing case: the dark referent, presented as a limit, not a win

*"Consider the role of dark referents."* / *"Wire this in."* (§7, S106, user standing.) A dark referent is a being that clears neither of `surfaces.js`'s two admission gates — the capitalisation test, which never starts a candidate run on "the cook," or the recurrence floor, off which a capitalised candidate can silently drop with zero trace — so it has *nothing* on the ledger anywhere, not even a typed `role:"void"` line, since void is typed only for a pronoun and a dark referent is nominal.

Four real specimens were found by grep against all 12 chapters of *Alice*, clean of any surface match: the jury (addressed by name, writes a verdict, chapters 11–12), the cook, the puppy, the hedgehog. The test (`eval/lavar/dark-referent-cluster-test.mjs`): for each specimen's hand-identified sibling mentions inside one chapter, cue the-fold's `Field` with each sibling's *own* sentence text — no name to cue with at all — and check whether the other siblings rank in the top-10%, against a matched random-whole-sentence control, the same beats-chance bar this project holds itself to everywhere else. **10 of 12 sibling pairs recovered (83.3%), against 1.53 expected by chance (12.8%) — the cue clearly beats the control, and 7 of the 10 hits clear the Field's own null-band significance margin.**

The charter is explicit about what this result is not: *"a PROPOSAL result only, per this session's own attention-proposes/priors-dispose discipline — it shows resemblance carries a real signal on a problem with no name to search with, not that the signal is calibrated enough to safely mint a referent and start binding content to it — that disposal step is real, scoped, and unbuilt."* This is the same posture as [Going and Looking](/going-and-looking): a result run to see if it would fail, that didn't fail, held to the standard of *not yet actionable* rather than inflated into a shipped capability the moment it clears a control.

## What refutes LaVar

The charter names its own falsifiers rather than leaving them implicit (§11). The ladder may not be a ladder — McGuffey graded for a child learning to decode, not a structural reader resolving referents, and a non-monotonic revision rate across rungs would mean the level column is meaningless. LaVar may be a second mouth — a frontier model pronouncing on referents is making claims, and claims from a model are exactly what the reader's provenance discipline exists to distrust by default (see [The Two Doors](/the-two-doors) and [The Model Is the Leaf](/the-model-is-the-leaf)): a LaVar revision that cannot ground in bytes is a typed gap, not a correction. And revision rate may not track comprehension at all — a rung where LaVar revises rarely may be a rung where the reader fails quietly, which is why the dashboard carries retrieval and cloze alongside revision rate, and a falling revision rate with flat comprehension should read as a warning, not progress.

What LaVar buys, stated at its own ceiling: it makes a wrong reading detectable and, over time, less frequent. It does not make a reading true. The Marmeladov specimen stays on file, archived rather than deleted, as the standing reminder of what a perfectly faithful projection of a wrong reading looks like (§12).
