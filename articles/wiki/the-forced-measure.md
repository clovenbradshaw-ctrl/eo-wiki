*Why the Born rule is the measure the ground demands, and what that does and does not license about the relation between EO, quantum structure, and cognition.*

## Statement

The Born rule is not imported into EO as an analogy to quantum mechanics. It is the measure the ground demands once the ground is read as a projective structure. Project a reading onto the twenty-seven cells, treat the projections as amplitudes rather than weights, square them, and normalize. That squared measure is not one option among many. At three values per axis it is the only consistent measure the geometry allows, by a theorem that predates all of this. The consequence is that EO's relation to quantum structure, and to cognition, is not resemblance. It is a shared constraint. This article states the claim at the level of adequacy the framework can defend now, and marks the stronger reading, that one algebra underlies both physics and mind, as the framework's most speculative tier by the framework's own accounting.

## The measure on the ground

A reading projects onto the cells of the ground. The projections are amplitudes, signed, and they are not the reading. The reading is the distribution got by squaring them and normalizing to one. Squaring suppresses the weak projections faster than the strong, quadratically, so a reading comes back sparse. Most cells carry almost no mass. A few carry most of it. The old habit of taking the single nearest cell was a hard collapse, a selection, the bivalent compression the framework already warns against. Keeping the squared distribution is the projection-preserving alternative, and it is the honest posture toward a truth the framework holds to be a limit approached rather than a value arrived at. A single collapsed reading claims to have arrived. A held distribution reports that it has not, and reports what it found on the way.

## Gleason and the trinary ground

Andrew Gleason proved in 1957 that for a projective structure of dimension three or higher, the squared-projection rule is the only consistent probability measure on the projections. Below three, at a single two-valued axis, other rules exist and the square is not forced. Recent expositions make the reason geometric: the loophole is open for a two-valued system and closes at three.

EO's axes carry three values each, minus one, plus one, and the square root of two. Each axis sits at the exact dimension where the measure stops being a choice. The wiki has named the number twenty-seven the framework's greatest theoretical vulnerability, because no theorem predicts it from first principles. Gleason predicts nothing about twenty-seven either. But it says something the framework has not yet used. Three per axis is the threshold below which a projective measure is arbitrary and at or above which it is unique. The trinary structure the framework arrived at for its own reasons is the structure at which a measure becomes forced rather than chosen.

This is suggestive and not proven, and the reason is precise. EO's centroids are learned points and are not in general an orthogonal projective basis, and overcomplete readings whose outcomes are not mutually exclusive are better described by positive operator valued measures than by the sharp projections Gleason governs. So the theorem motivates the square here. It does not transfer to the ground as a proof. What is forced is what a genuine projective structure of this dimension would demand. Whether the ground is projective enough to inherit the demand is itself a thing to measure.

## Consilience, not analogy

Three independent traditions reach this same geometry from outside EO, which is the pattern the framework's consilience commitment predicts.

Information retrieval reached it first. Van Rijsbergen, in 2004, represented documents as vectors, relevance as an observable, and cosine similarity as a projection operator, and grounded the probability assignment with Gleason. Widdows built the companion on the logic side, reading negation as orthogonality in a space of word meaning.

Cognition reached it next. Busemeyer, Bruza, Pothos, and the Brussels group model human judgment as projective measurement, and rest the case on two signatures classical probability cannot produce. The first is order effects, the fact that two questions asked in different orders give different answers, upheld across seventy national surveys and read as the non-commutativity of the measured observables. The second is interference in combination, the Pet-Fish or guppy effect, where a combination carries typicality that neither part supplies. In Aerts' reading the interference term is not a reasoning error. It is the mark that a new whole has emerged from the combination. That is EO's root-two emergence in another vocabulary.

None of this is EO borrowing from physics. These are other doors into the same room. The framework's consilience claim is exactly this, that the domains are subject to the same constraints on coherent transformation, and that those constraints generate a shared grammar of appearance. The forced measure is one such constraint, and its appearance in retrieval, in cognition, and in physics is one structure expressed several times rather than several resemblances.

## The strong reading, and its status

The radical reading goes further than shared constraint. It holds that quantum states and cognition are not merely both describable by a projective measure but are manifestations of one underlying algebra, of which EO's operators are a candidate. This reading has serious company. Aerts holds the ontological version, the conceptuality interpretation, in which quantum entities have a conceptual nature and physical processes are the counterpart of the meaning processes that occur in cognition. Coecke holds the formal version, categorical quantum mechanics, in which grammar and quantum process share one category and physics and language are functors out of it.

In EO's own taxonomy this is a Newtonian claim. It asserts that the operators reflect the real structure of transformation, not only that they save the appearances. The framework's standing discipline is that the Newtonian claim is earned only after expressive completeness is established, and that it may not be the right frame at all. So the strong reading is legitimate as a conjecture, and it sits at the framework's most speculative tier, by the framework's own standard, not by an outside objection.

The defensible tier is the Ptolemaic one. Both domains obey the same transformation constraints, those constraints generate one grammar, and Gleason shows the grammar forces the same measure in any sufficient realization. That is already more than resemblance. It flips the direction of explanation away from cognition imitating physics, without asserting the causal claim. It is one structure expressed twice. The two tiers should not be welded. The adequacy claim can stand while the ontological claim remains open, and welding them is how a defensible result gets discredited by an ambitious one.

## What would earn the strong reading

The objection to beat is that a projective or compact closed structure describes many theories, not only quantum theory. Categorical quantum mechanics uses this deliberately, to isolate the ingredient that makes quantum theory quantum against foils such as Spekkens' toy theory. So sharing the generic scaffold is weak. The content is whether EO shares the specific ingredient. Four tests move the claim from projection sketch toward demonstration, and each is buildable and each can come back negative.

First, non-commutativity recovered natively. If the faces, or the significance operators, are genuinely order-dependent measurements, EO carries one of quantum theory's defining ingredients without importing it. This is the single most decisive test.

Second, the measure derived rather than applied. Show that the ground is a projective structure of dimension three, and Gleason forces the square from within, which is EO generating the Born rule rather than borrowing it. The three-per-axis fact is the opening for this.

Third, a shared prohibition. The empty cell at Synthesis by Ground is forbidden in language, zero verbs across the corpus. A permission shared across domains can be coincidence. A prohibition shared across domains is far harder to arrange. If the shared algebra is real, the quantum structure should forbid the corresponding operation in the same place.

Fourth, emergence as composition. Root two, the emergence value, read as the interference term in cognition and as the composition structure in the categorical account, would be one shared ingredient rather than three restatements of it.

## What EO adds

The prior art supplies the thesis but leaves the shared algebra generic. Coecke names it a compact closed category. Aerts names it conceptuality. EO's contribution, if the tests hold, is a specific candidate: nine operators over a trinary ground, with the significance triad as a candidate measurement cycle, definition preparing the terms, evaluation rendering the judgment, recursion rotating the frame.

The form of the claim to hold is the symmetric one. Neither physics nor cognition is primary. Both are realizations of a prior algebra. This asks less than the interpretations that make one domain explain the other, and it is harder to refute, because it never has to claim that minds explain particles or particles explain minds. It has only to exhibit the shared algebra and the ingredient that specializes it in each domain.

## Status

Adequacy, defensible now: the Born rule is the forced measure on a projective reading of the ground, and its appearance across retrieval, cognition, and physics is a shared constraint rather than an analogy.

Suggestive: the coincidence between three values per axis and Gleason's dimension threshold, where a projective measure passes from arbitrary to unique.

Projection sketch: the identification of EO's operator algebra as the specific shared structure, and the significance triad as a measurement cycle.

Untested: the four tests above, non-commutativity, the internal derivation of the measure, the shared prohibition, and emergence as composition. Each is buildable. Each is built to be able to say no.
