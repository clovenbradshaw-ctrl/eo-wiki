*Ant colonies build nests without an architect: an ant that finds food deposits a trail, later ants follow the strongest trail, their returns deposit again, and the short path wins without anyone computing it. [Emergent Organizing](/emergent-organizing) names this pattern — SIG(environment) → INS(trace) → REC(trace, next-action) — and illustrates it with ants, Wikipedia, and Linux. It cites no code, because when that article was written there was none to cite. There now is.*

## The bug the pattern was supposed to fix

`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md` opens with an incident, not a theory: gemma2:2b asked "who is president?" answers from parametric memory, states it with full confidence, and the answer ships to the chat window with zero mechanical veto (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:3-6`). Two bypass points let this happen, and both are named, not inferred, because someone read `proxy-runner.mjs` to find them: a small-talk fast path whose regex gate (`isSmallTalk`, `proxy-runner.mjs:4440-4445`) classifies only the *shape* of the query — greeting, acknowledgment, name-exchange — and says nothing about whether the answer it produces asserts a checkable claim; and a main path whose reading-surface verdict machinery was computed but never read before the text shipped (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:83-87`).

The honest diagnosis, stated in the design doc before any fix is proposed, is worth quoting because it is the sentence that keeps the rest of this article from overclaiming:

> "The ladder as it exists today can only catch a claim that conflicts with material the session itself pulled in this turn — it was built for 'does the answer match what we just read,' not 'is this current-affairs fact still true.'" (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:150-153`)

Closing that gap required either a live current-facts source wired in as a witness, or accepting a narrower floor: the honest admission that a claim has no ground (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:155-167`). The floor shipped first — an `unsourced-current` verdict and an appended disclaimer. Then, per the amendment dated 2026-09-19, the mechanism grew past the floor into something considerably more interesting: a live current-facts witness, built as a general kind-and-link structure rather than a "who is president" special case, resolved through the stigmergic routing this article is about.

## Kinds, and the state of a kind is a dated link

The first move is a modeling decision that keeps the whole mechanism general. A role word like "president" is not stored as a row with a `current_holder` column that gets overwritten. It is a **kind** — `kind:role:president`, addressable in the hyperlexicon exactly the way any discovered kind is (`kindNotes()`, `native/kernel/hyperlexicon-routes.js:96-109`) — and the kind's state at any moment is a **link**: a dated arrangement `kindId --holds-office--> holder`, carrying `since`/`until` (`native/kernel/hyperlexicon-routes.js:12-20`). The two shapes are declared at the top of the module:

```
kind: { id, label, jurisdiction?, aliases: [], parameters: [], memberOf: [] }
link: { id, kindId, holder, since?, until?, at, ref, query?, giver, ordinal? }
```

The moment any door dates a link — Wikidata's P580/P582 start/end qualifiers via `native/organs/current-holder.js:23-35`, or a "since … term ending …" ground the text itself carries — the kind gains the `has-term` parameter and membership in the meta-kind `kind:has-term` (`withTermStructure`, `native/kernel/hyperlexicon-routes.js:88-93`). "Current" then reads structurally: `currentLinkOf` picks the link whose interval contains `now`, falling back to the most recently refreshed link when none covers it exactly (`native/kernel/hyperlexicon-routes.js:165-175`). Nothing in this file has ever seen the word "president" as anything other than one label among others; the same structure would serve "mayor," "CEO," or "the current officeholder of any office a door can date."

Five mechanical hops resolve a request against this structure — `exact-kind`, `alias`, `jurisdiction`, `ordinal`, `holder` (`native/kernel/hyperlexicon-routes.js:34-40`) — and a request matching none of them returns the honest absence, `kind: null`, never a guess.

## The pheromone layer, made pure

The order those five hops are tried in is not fixed. It is learned, and the learning mechanism is `native/kernel/stigmergy.js`, whose header states its own debt plainly:

> "No ant plans the colony's route. Each ant that returns from a food source deposits a chemical trail in the shared environment; later ants read the deposits and follow the strongest; their own returns deposit again — positive feedback, evaporation, and the short path wins without anyone having computed it." (`native/kernel/stigmergy.js:3-7`, Handle: Wilson, after E.O. Wilson's *The Ants*)

The module is explicit about what it does and does not know: "Nothing here knows what a route is about, what a 'president' is, or who asked. It is stigmergy, not a fact table" (`native/kernel/stigmergy.js:12-13`). A **deposit** is one resolution attempt — `{ head, route, ok, ms, at }` — appended to a per-head trail, capped at `TRAIL_WINDOW = 128` so the oldest deposits fall out of the ledger itself, not just out of the weighting (`native/kernel/stigmergy.js:40,56-60`). **Evaporation** is exponential decay from each deposit's own timestamp — `weight = exp(-ageDays / halfLifeDays)`, with a default half-life of exactly seven days (`TRAIL_HALF_LIFE_DAYS = 7`, `native/kernel/stigmergy.js:38,45-48`). The **learned order** for a head sorts routes by summed weighted strength, breaks ties by mean latency — "compared for speed," the module's own phrase (`native/kernel/stigmergy.js:62`) — and lets routes with zero deposits fall back to the structural default order (`routeOrderFor`, `native/kernel/stigmergy.js:83-97`). A small exploration constant, `EXPLORE_EPSILON = 0.1`, lets a never-tried route be promoted to the front with 10% probability regardless of how strong the learned order has become — Wilson's scouts, injectable and reproducible for tests via an explicit `rng` argument (`native/kernel/stigmergy.js:41,96`).

This is where [Emergent Organizing](/emergent-organizing)'s abstract loop — SIG(environment) → INS(trace) → REC(trace, next-action) — stops being a metaphor and becomes a specific claim about a specific function. `depositTrail` is INS: it instantiates one trace record from one resolution attempt. `routeOrderFor` is REC in the strict sense [Holons](/holons) gives the operator: not changing a value, but "reframing the ground from which change proceeds" — the *order in which future routing happens* is itself recomputed from the accumulated trace, every time. Holons' own account of REC notes that its Ground-facing cell (position 22, "Dialectics") is the sparsest in the entire 27-cell capacity ground — fifteen verbs, empty in seventeen of twenty-seven languages, because "the language for it barely exists" (`holons.md`). `routeOrderFor` is that exact operation with no natural-language name at all: forty-odd lines of JavaScript that reframe how the colony will search next, based on nothing but what the colony has already found.

## The swarm, and one falsification

A design that claims collective learning is not evidence of collective learning. `native/kernel/swarm-collective-learning.test.mjs` runs five falsifiable properties against the real `stigmergy.js` + `hyperlexicon-routes.js` + `current-facts.js` stack — "no model — no LaVar" — every agent a seeded, mechanical asker against one shared environment file (`native/kernel/swarm-collective-learning.test.mjs:1-31`).

**Convergence is measured, not merely claimed to fall.** Re-running the exact SWARM-1 harness (six epochs, twenty seeded agents per epoch, exploiting the learned order with no scouting) prints mean probes per resolution of **1.450 in epoch 0, then flat at 1.000 for every subsequent epoch** — the colony converges to the direct alias route after a single epoch of twenty agents, not gradually across the run. The design document's own amendment describes this as "1.45 -> 1.00 mean probes over 8 epochs" (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:278-279`); the shipped test runs six epochs (`EPOCHS = 6`, `swarm-collective-learning.test.mjs:68`), and the convergence it measures is a step, not a gradual descent — a small but real discrepancy between the design note's prose and what the harness that survived actually asserts (`first > last` and `last < 1.25`, `swarm-collective-learning.test.mjs:82-83`).

**Transfer** needs no repetition: agent A adopts a dated record no one else has seen; agent B, who never calls a door, resolves correctly off A's deposit alone (`swarm-collective-learning.test.mjs:89-99`).

**Amplification and evaporation** are both measured on the same trail. Ten successful resolutions through an alias sum to strength greater than 9 (near-linear reinforcement); the identical ten trails, forty days later, weigh under 1 — evaporated below a single fresh success on a competing hop, so the colony switches which hop it tries first purely because traffic moved (`swarm-collective-learning.test.mjs:101-117`).

**The falsification is the one that matters most.** Positive feedback has no opinion about truth. SWARM 4 seeds the colony's *first* deposit with the wrong answer — Joe Biden, dated 2021, instead of the actual current holder — then runs eight resolutions. Every one of them resolves to the wrong holder; the gate's own confidence follows the environment, not the facts, and a *correct* model draft ("Donald Trump.") is struck and replaced with the wrong sentence, verbatim: `"As of 2021-01-20, the president of the United States is Joe Biden."` (`swarm-collective-learning.test.mjs:119-133`). The wrong route's strength climbs past 7 — amplified by the very traffic that is wrong. This is collective mislearning, real and reproduced on demand, and the test says so without hedging: "the gate ships the wrong value with full mechanical confidence" (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:282-283`). The only thing that stops it is `markVetoed`, an explicit alarm — not a retry, not a majority vote, an event of its own type, appended to the same trail ledger with `route: "veto"` (`native/organs/current-facts.js:319-329`). After the veto, the vetoed link is suspended for `VETO_SUSPICION_DAYS = 14` (`native/organs/current-facts.js:49`), the colony's next resolution comes back `ok: false` rather than confidently wrong, and only a fresh door call — the periodic refresh described below — clears the suspicion. Stigmergy without a veto channel is a system that launders confidence out of repetition; this is the disclosed limit of the pattern, not swept under the swarm's own success.

**The scouts never stop.** Even after the colony has a strong learned order, `EXPLORE_EPSILON` keeps probing: across sixty resolutions drawn under conditions that favor exploration, the never-tried route is promoted to the front at least ten times (`swarm-collective-learning.test.mjs:145-155`).

One genuine inefficiency was found and fixed by this same swarm, not designed away in advance: the `holder` hop preempted unconditionally, so an alias head that is also caps-shaped like a holder name — "POTUS" — paid a wasted probe on the holder hop on *every single resolution, forever*, even after the colony had thoroughly learned the alias route. The fix, visible in the shipped code's own comment, restricts that preemption to "only while the colony hasn't learned" — once trails exist under the head, the learned order governs instead (`native/kernel/hyperlexicon-routes.js:293-298`). A stigmergic system audited its own routing policy and found a standing tax that a fixed rule had been silently charging.

## Contest, never overwrite

The alias mechanism itself was rebuilt mid-project on explicit user direction ("that should resolve by referent using the hyperlexicon," recorded in `GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:292-293`) to stop being a string field on a kind and become an **admission** in the hyperlexicon's own ledger: `(POTUS, is-alias-of, kind:role:president)`, the same act shape every other assertion in the system uses. `falsify-referents.test.mjs` is what happened when that rebuild was put under fire, and it found one real bug.

Two admissions can bind the same surface word to two *different* referents — a stale door's write and a foreign write disagreeing about what "POTUS" means. Before the fix, this produced silent last-writer-wins in the route index and first-writer-wins in `kindFor` simultaneously: two different readers of the same environment would get two different, confident, silently disagreeing answers (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:308-312`). The fix is disclosure, not arbitration: `hearAlias` refuses the second, conflicting admission outright, typed `contested-surface`, naming the binding already on file (`native/kernel/falsify-referents.test.mjs:43-56`); the route's own guard then excludes any contested surface from resolution entirely — it resolves to *nothing*, the honest ambiguity, rather than picking a winner (`native/kernel/falsify-referents.test.mjs:58-72`). The two notes are never deleted; the disagreement stays on the record, typed, exactly as [The Two Doors](/the-two-doors) describes the corroboration organ's own `dispute()` act refusing to convict either side of a contradiction it cannot adjudicate: "at two sources you can see a disagreement but not who is wrong." Two independently built parts of the same codebase arrived at the identical doctrine — a contest is a fact to disclose, never a coin to flip — one for contested facts (`organs/corroboration.js`, Handle: Bukhari) and one for contested aliases (`kernel/hyperlexicon-routes.js`'s `contested` index). Neither cites the other.

Two further disciplines were enforced by the same suite, both load-bearing rather than decorative. Every admission must name its giver: a witness-less alias binding is refused with reason `no-witness`, and a batch call (`upsertKind`) discloses that refusal rather than dropping it silently (`native/kernel/falsify-referents.test.mjs:82-92`) — a direct instance of the constitutional requirement (II.2) that a prior without a giver is a typed gap, never a silently accepted one. And the per-surface trail was *probed as a suspected bug and refuted*: the natural instinct is to share a learned route order per referent (all the ways of asking about the presidency learn together), but the alias hop is surface-specific — "POTUS" reaches the kind through the alias note, "president" reaches it directly — so a referent-shared order would force the head surface to pay the alias hop's cost on every resolution forever. The test that checks this is explicitly framed as a refutation of its own initial suspicion (`native/kernel/falsify-referents.test.mjs:106-120`), which is the falsification discipline this wiki elsewhere calls for in the abstract, here applied to one design decision inside one module.

## The surgical gate: strike, don't refuse

`native/organs/fact-gate.js`'s `applyVerdictGate` is where the kind-and-link structure and the stigmergic route meet the model's actual draft text. Its four verdicts are exhaustive and typed: `grounded` (the draft's committed values match the link), `contradicted` (the draft names a value the link does not hold), `unsourced-current` (an open-now claim with no dated ground reachable at all), `untouched` (no flaggable claim — the caller's own path continues) (`native/organs/fact-gate.js:369-376`).

The design decision that matters is *strike-and-splice*, never whole-turn refusal: "most answers have one flagged sentence among several fine ones... Sentence-level strike + computed fallback matches the ladder's own granularity" (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:122-126`), the same pattern the codebase's `firewall.js` already uses for a different problem (`strikeAddresses`, `native/organs/firewall.js:155`). And the replacement sentence is never model-phrased. It is assembled mechanically from the kind's label (the frame), the link's holder (the value), and the link's own date — never from the draft's words (`native/organs/fact-gate.js:483-498`, `GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:128-132`). "Direct application of 'model is just the mouth'" is the design doc's own gloss, and it is the same discipline [The Model Is the Leaf](/the-model-is-the-leaf) documents for the contract system generally: the model is never asked to write its own correction, because that reopens exactly the door the gate exists to close. The gate is wired at both call sites the original bug report named — the fast path (`proxy-runner.mjs:4489`, right after the small-talk regex at `4440-4445`) and the main path (`proxy-runner.mjs:8146`) — closing both bypasses the design doc opened with.

Stale links do not sit forever: `refreshStale` re-checks any link whose term has lapsed or that has never carried one, once that link's own refresh date (`at`) is more than 30 days old (`LINK_TTL_DAYS = 30`, `native/organs/current-facts.js:48`), on a boot delay plus a recurring six-hour interval (`FACTS_REFRESH_MS = 6 * 60 * 60 * 1000`, `proxy-runner.mjs:478,489-490`). A link whose term still covers `now` is exempt from that TTL — it is left alone, no matter its age, unless it has been vetoed: "the alarm is exactly the signal that the door must be asked again" (`native/organs/current-facts.js:338-339`).

## One kernel, eleven grammars

The gate's closed-class grammar — wh-words, present-tense copulas, the definite article, the "of" preposition — is English. That fact was named directly rather than left implicit, under a stated directive: "GFP is our grammar" (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:211`). The fix is `native/adapters/text/fact-lenses.js`: a **lens** per language declares the same closed classes and the mouth's own assembly templates, so the kernel in `fact-gate.js` stays language-blind and every function there accepts an optional `lens` argument, defaulting to the English lens when omitted (byte-identical behavior for every existing caller). `FACT_LENSES` currently holds exactly eleven lenses — eng, spa, fra, deu, ita, por, rus, arb, jpn, hin, swa (`native/adapters/text/fact-lenses.js:74-177`) — confirming the pitch's "eleven languages" claim by direct count of the object, not by trusting a comment.

The lens carries real orthographic knowledge, not a uniform template. German's `capsAreAnchor: false` flag exists because German capitalizes every noun, so capitalization cannot be read as English reads it — a proper-noun anchor there (`native/adapters/text/fact-lenses.js:112`). `lensForAsk` detects which lens applies from the ask's own wh-opener, falling back to a first-character probe for no-space scripts, and returns `null` — no grammar at all — for an opener no lens holds (`native/adapters/text/fact-lenses.js:192-207`). That `null` is not a bug to patch; it is the mechanism's declared safe-failure arm, exercised on Korean, Greek, Thai, Welsh, Vietnamese, and Polish in the battery below, every one of them asserted `untouched` — never struck, never given an English sentence.

## The seventy-five questions, and what they actually falsified

`native/organs/falsify-omnilingual.test.mjs` is the closing check, and it disagrees with its own header comment in a way worth stating plainly, because catching a wrong comment against the actual array is exactly the discipline this wiki asks writers to apply to *itself*: the file's opening line claims "SEVENTY-FIVE questions in twenty-four languages" (`falsify-omnilingual.test.mjs:1`), but counting the distinct language tags in the `ROWS` array itself — done directly against the file, not against the comment — gives **75 rows across exactly 17 distinct languages** (eng, spa, fra, deu, ita, por, rus, arb, jpn, hin, swa, kor, ell, tha, cym, vie, pol), matching the pitch's "17-language" figure and not the module's own header. Running the suite passes all three tests it contains (`node --test`, verified live), so the discrepancy is cosmetic — a stale comment, not a wrong assertion — but it is the kind of thing that only surfaces by reading the array instead of the prose above it.

The battery's letter-lanes are falsifiers with declared intents, not a smoke test: (A) the same kernel produces the same verdict shape across eng/spa/fra/deu/ita; (C) an ordinal ask ("the 46th president") never falls through to today's link — specificity is a lock, not a preference (`native/kernel/hyperlexicon-routes.js:288-298`); (E) a scoped ask with no matching kind in the environment misses honestly rather than answering with an unscoped kind's link; (F) case-marked and article-free languages (Russian, Arabic, Japanese, Hindi, Swahili) are never struck even when a kind exists, because the lens genuinely cannot type the claim's scope; (K) a definitional sentence with a capitalized complement ("The president is the Head of State.") is correctly read as a definition, never a claim; (P) an alias learned in English never resolves in a French ask — "Qui est POTUS ?" fails honestly in a French-only environment (`falsify-omnilingual.test.mjs:187-193`).

Three real bugs were found by running this battery against the live code, not hypothesized in advance, and the design doc names all three without softening:

1. **`extractHolder`'s capital-letter class was ASCII `[A-Z]`** — a Cyrillic holder's name never matched at all. Fixed to `\p{Lu}`, the Unicode uppercase category.
2. **`\b` word-boundary regex is ASCII-anchored even with the `/u` flag** — a recurring lesson the project's own notes flag by name (LAVAR S103) — replaced with explicit bounded lookaheads.
3. **A wh-word was read as a holder name in Cyrillic asks** — "Кто" ("who") was, for one code path, treated as a capitalized proper noun candidate rather than recognized as grammar. The fix: the wh-opener is grammar, never a person (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:236-241`).

A fourth and fifth bug surfaced in the 75-row battery specifically rather than the earlier lens-tier work: German's genitive complement — "der Präsident der Vereinigten Staaten" — had its second article (the complement's own "der") unrecognized as an anchor, so the head-walk swallowed the whole phrase and reported the head as "Vereinigten." The fix generalizes rather than patches: an article always begins a new noun phrase, so the walk now stops at any article (`native/organs/fact-gate.js:96-107`). And a French definite complement — "Le président est le chef de l'État." — was struck because `l'État` read as a committed value; the fix is the **complement-article rule**: a role mention before the copula is a holder claim only when the post-copula complement is a bare name with no article, because a definite complement is a definition, never a claim (`native/organs/fact-gate.js:405-419`, `GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:267-271`).

## The ceiling, disclosed

The mechanism's own limits are stated as part of the design, not discovered afterward as an embarrassment. There is no cross-language resolution: "president" does not resolve "président," by construction, and `extractHolder` reads English ground patterns only — "if those tests ever pass, the mechanism has silently learned to translate, which would be a guess" (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:242-245`). Aliases are never invented, only learned from an explicit admission with a named giver. A contested surface resolves to nothing rather than a majority answer. An unlensed language is read by no grammar at all rather than falling back to English's. And the honesty floor beneath all of it has not moved since the very first amendment: "No door, no link: the draft's open-now claim is replaced by 'I don't have a grounded source for this as of \<date\>; my training data may be stale on this point.' The system cannot invent the right answer; it can refuse to ship a guess" (`GATE-STALE-FACTS-DESIGN.md:204-207`).

That sentence is the entire article in miniature, and it is worth setting beside [The Two Doors](/the-two-doors)' own law — "priors may condition orientation... they cannot become witness merely by being prior" — because a language model's parametric memory of who the president was in its training window is exactly a prior in that sense. It can nominate a candidate. Stigmergic routing over kinds and links is what decides, mechanically and on the record, whether that candidate ever becomes something the system will say out loud.
