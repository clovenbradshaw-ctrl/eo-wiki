*Every organ elsewhere in this wiki reads text. These three watch the box that is doing the reading — and route what they find through the same admission gate the text-reading swarm already earned the hard way.*

## The loop, stated once

`native/organs/apollo.js`'s own header names five steps: **sense → baseline → surprise → investigate → remedy** (`apollo.js:1-8`). Apollo does the first three, `apollo-swarm.js` the fourth, `thea.js` the fifth. A sixth step — *act* — belongs to none of them. Thea's own header is explicit about the boundary: "Thea never executes; the caller (proxy/heimdall) does" (`thea.js:8-9`) — and by the end of this article that omission will look load-bearing, not accidental.

The organ states its own restraint plainly: "PURE: no I/O, no clock reads except caller-supplied `at`... the caller declares k, window, and hysteresis" (`apollo.js:10-12`). This is the discipline [Nine Instructions](/nine-instructions) describes for EO's contract system generally — a part gets exactly the authority it needs and nothing ambient — run here on a domain that has nothing to do with parsing a sentence: CPU idle percentage, outbound call counts, generated-token counts. The operator algebra is not only a reading grammar. In this file it is watching the vital signs of the process doing the reading.

## Apollo: five declared channels, one statistic, no invented threshold

`APOLLO_CHANNELS` is a closed, frozen array of exactly five names — `turnMs`, `cpuIdle`, `apiCalls`, `genTokens`, `refusals` (`apollo.js:14-20`). Nothing outside that set can be watched: `observe()` refuses an unknown channel with a typed reason, "channel is not one of APOLLO_CHANNELS; declare it before watching it" (`apollo.js:22-26`). Apollo also refuses to render a verdict on a first impression — under `minSamples` (default 10) observations, every sample returns `kind: "learning"`, never `ok` or `alarm` (`apollo.js:55`) — the same case `APOLLO_REFUSALS.thin_baseline` names ("surprise needs a baseline, not a first impression"), though that constant is declared but never itself returned on this path: a `learning` finding carries no `refused` field at all.

The baseline itself is an exponentially-weighted moving mean and variance, folded in one sample at a time (`updateStat`, `apollo.js:38-43`), at a default rate `alpha = 0.15` (`apollo.js:32`). A sample is *surprising* when its z-score against the *previous* baseline's standard deviation clears `k` (default 3) sigma (`apollo.js:56-58`) — the comparison is deliberately against the baseline as it stood before this sample, so the sample cannot smear the baseline it is being judged against. A second, sharper category, *runaway*, requires the sample to already be surprising **and** more than four times the mean **and** still growing (`apollo.js:59-60`) — explosive generation registers differently from a slow drift, because the two call for different remedies later.

Between "surprising" and "acted on" sits hysteresis: a single surprising sample is only ever `watch`; `alarm` fires only once the streak of consecutive surprises reaches the caller's declared `hysteresis` (default 2) (`apollo.js:69-73`). The comment is explicit about why: "never flaps" (`apollo.js:31`). `apollo.test.mjs:15-24` pins the shape directly — twelve quiet `apiCalls` samples, then a spike to 30 returns `watch` with `surpriseOf(w) === null`, and only a second sustained value of 45 returns `alarm`. `surpriseOf()` is the admission gate at Apollo's own exit: only `alarm` and `runaway` are dispatch-grade (`apollo.js:77-82`); `ok`, `watch`, and `learning` never reach the swarm at all. This is a cheap filter placed in front of an expensive one.

## eoSwarm: the operator algebra, rerun on vitals instead of verbs

`apollo-swarm.js` is marked, in its own second line, as a "server-side bridge (imports eo-swarm + organs; NOT in the browser seam)" — and indeed `native/organs/index.js` re-exports Apollo and Thea's pure functions at its browser-safe seam but pointedly not the dispatch bridge, "server-side only — import by path" (`native/organs/index.js:150-151`). Remedy-breeding is a capability the browser build never gets.

`REMEDY_HYPOTHESES` is five fixed strings — `throttle-caller`, `failover-model`, `re-forge-cache`, `defer-batch`, `downgrade-mind` (`apollo-swarm.js:12-14`) — the entire search space `dispatch()` breeds over. Each is wrapped as an "ant" carrying a real cube coordinate: `op` cycles through `["SEG", "CON", "DEF", "EVA", "REC"][i % 5]` at `grain: "Figure"` (`apollo-swarm.js:27-30`), so the mapping is exact and fixed — `throttle-caller`↔SEG, `failover-model`↔CON, `re-forge-cache`↔DEF, `defer-batch`↔EVA, `downgrade-mind`↔REC. Worth being honest about what that tagging buys: `eo-swarm.mjs`'s own header says the mechanism "knows nothing about reading organs, chapters, or text — only genotype ids, a caller-supplied fitness, and the cube coordinates a caller attaches to its own ants for persona/stance labeling." The op/grain pair drives `stanceOf`/`personaOf` labeling (which archon-flavored "stance" a hypothesis gets called), not a claim that throttling a caller *is*, in any deeper sense, an act of Segmentation. The Handle color is downstream of the tag, not the reason for it.

The gate itself is the genuinely load-bearing part, and it is not new machinery built for this file. `dispatch()` calls `eoSwarm({ ants, fitness, bar, terrainOf: () => [surprise.channel], legal: () => true })` (`apollo-swarm.js:31-35`) — `fitness` and `bar` are required, caller-supplied, never hand-set inside the organ (`apollo-swarm.js:25-26`), the identical discipline Apollo itself observes for `k`/`hysteresis`. `eoSwarm()` is `native/eval/lavar/eo-swarm.mjs`'s composition over `createSwarmGate` from `native/eval/lavar/swarm-gate.mjs` — and that is **the same function the text-reading pipeline's own evolution loop calls**: `wilson.mjs:60` imports `createSwarmGate` from the identical module, and `wilson.mjs:354` instantiates it with `ELENCHUS_BAR`. One shared admission gate serves two unrelated domains — reading-organ recombination and remedy-hypothesis recombination — through the same acceptance test.

That gate is worth trusting because it has already been caught wrong twice, on the record. `elenchus-bar.mjs`'s header recounts a hand-set `+0.005` acceptance threshold that turned out to be "FIVE ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE above" the material's own reproducibility floor — 21 different genotypes on one chapter produced the identical 16-digit shape `0.583042748288777`, so the honest noise floor was ~0, and the hand-set bar refused every candidate: "302 births, 0 kept." `swarm-gate.mjs`'s header documents a second, independent defect in the same neighborhood — the admission check was comparing a candidate's *rereading correction* (`delta`), not its *improvement over the best*, so a genuinely better variant (`noun-phrase-subjects 0.574 vs best 0.561`) was refused anyway: "416 births, 0 kept." Both were fixed by replacing the hand-set number with `bornAcceptance()`'s measured rerun-null floor, not by relaxing anyone's judgment call. `apollo-swarm.dispatch()` inherits that fix for free, which is the actual weight behind calling it "the same admission gate the reading pipeline uses" rather than a superficial resemblance.

Of `eoSwarm()`'s two faces, one is exercised here and the other is not. BREED (CON·Figure) unions every unordered pair of the five seed ants — ten pairs — into a candidate remedy set, admits it only if it improves over its terrain's champion by at least `bar` and clears `bornAcceptance` (`eo-swarm.mjs:122-137`), so a dispatch can genuinely surface a *composite* remedy like `{"throttle-caller","failover-model"}` if the caller's fitness function says so. DIFFERENTIATE (SEG·Figure, `specialistsOf`) needs `depsOf` and `terrainOfOrgan`, and `apollo-swarm.js`'s call to `eoSwarm()` supplies neither (`apollo-swarm.js:31-35`) — so remedy-breeding only ever recombines, it never splits a remedy into terrain specialists. That is a real, disclosed limit of this wiring, not a bug: `terrainOf` here is always the single channel that surprised, so there is only ever one terrain to specialize into in the first place.

## Thea: verdicts into seven words, and never her own hand on the switch

`THEA_ACTIONS` is a closed vocabulary of exactly seven strings: `pace`, `defer`, `downgrade`, `escalate`, `re-forge`, `quarantine-caller`, `observe` (`thea.js:11-13`). The file's own comment claims these come from "Heimdall's own homeostatic vocabulary," and that claim checks out precisely rather than loosely: `heimdall.mjs`'s header for "HEIMDALL THE SENTINEL: the outward flows" says the sentinel "regulates the box homeostatically — sense the vitals, compare to a setpoint, act (pace / defer / escalate / downgrade / re-forge) to restore equilibrium BEFORE breakdown, with hysteresis so he never flaps" (`heimdall.mjs:4062-4069`) — five of Thea's seven words, verbatim, describing a *different* homeostatic loop (Heimdall routes outward vision/reasoning API calls across model paths; Apollo watches in-process vitals). Thea's two additions have no Heimdall precedent in that file: `quarantine-caller` (rationing one specific misbehaving requestor, narrower than Heimdall's path-level `escalate`) and `observe` (a deliberate non-action). Worth naming plainly: the correspondence is between two prose headers, not a shared imported constant — nothing in the tree forces `THEA_ACTIONS` and `heimdall.mjs`'s comment to stay in lockstep if either changes.

`craftRemedy({ finding, report })` is pure and refuses in exactly two typed ways: no finding (`no_finding`) or no swarm report (`no_report`) — "remedy without investigation is a guess" (`thea.js:15-18`), and `apollo.test.mjs:57-58` pins the second refusal directly. The branch logic is genuinely keyed to channel semantics, not a generic switch: `runaway` on `genTokens` pushes `defer` ("hold new turns until the box recovers") and `downgrade` ("drop to a cheaper mind while generation is unbounded"); `runaway` on `apiCalls` pushes `quarantine-caller` then `pace`; `alarm` on `turnMs`/`cpuIdle` pushes `pace` then `defer`; `alarm` on `refusals` pushes only `downgrade` (`thea.js:34-43`). The swarm's own verdict, not just Apollo's raw finding, steers the output: `has("failover-model")` — checking whether that hypothesis survived into `report.best.ids` — is what licenses `escalate`, and `has("throttle-caller")` is what licenses `quarantine-caller` on an `apiCalls` alarm (`thea.js:38-39, 43, 45`). A finding whose `kind` is anything else (reachable only if `craftRemedy` is called directly, since `dispatch()` already filters through `surpriseOf` before ever reaching Thea) falls to `observe`, with its own disclosed reason: "is not dispatch-grade: watch, do not act" (`thea.js:47`) — defense-in-depth for a caller that bypasses the loop's own gate, not a path this loop exercises itself.

Every remedy carries a one-line, human-readable audit string naming the kind, channel, z-score to two decimals, the actions taken, and the hypotheses examined (`thea.js:50`) — the same "one audit trail" instinct [Nine Instructions](/nine-instructions) attributes to hoisting validation into the algebra generally. And the returned object's own `standing` is `"proposed"` (`thea.js:51`), pointedly not `"witnessed"` — Apollo's own `alarm`/`runaway` findings *do* carry `standing: "witnessed"` (`apollo.js:66, 72`). That distinction is [The Two Doors](/the-two-doors)'s doctrine showing up in a different organ: a witnessed finding is evidence anchored to an actual sampled vital; a proposed remedy is a nomination the caller may decline. Thea's own comment says she "never executes; the caller (proxy/heimdall) does" (`thea.js:8-9`), and the codebase backs that up in a way worth stating without softening: a repo-wide search for `dispatch(` outside `apollo-swarm.js`'s own definition turns up exactly one caller, `apollo.test.mjs`. As of this reading, the sense→baseline→surprise→investigate→remedy loop is real, pure, and tested end to end; the sixth step — a live process actually calling `dispatch()` and acting on what Thea proposes — has no committed call site in production code. That is the same kind of disclosed, not-yet-wired gap [The Two Doors](/the-two-doors) names for `perturbation-challenger.js`: shipped, tested, importable, and waiting for a caller.

## Where this loop ends, and where Heimdall's begins

It is tempting to fold Apollo's loop into [Heimdall, Out of the Sandbox](/heimdall-out-of-the-sandbox)'s fleet — both are homeostatic, both use the word "hysteresis," both propose rather than unilaterally kill. They should stay distinct, because they answer different failure classes. The fleet exists because of a specific, dated incident: `proxy.mjs` wedged in an infinite microtask self-loop on 2026-09-19, 98.5% CPU for roughly ten hours, unreachable on `/health`, deaf to `SIGTERM` — and the watcher that should have caught it was running *inside* the process that wedged, so it wedged too (`native/docs/HEIMDALL-FLEET.md`). Its fix is architectural: a separate OS process, `heimdall-fleet.mjs` on its own port, watching self and peers, raising every termination decision to a human operator who must say yes — nothing in that design executes a remedy on its own authority either, but the escalation path ends at a person, not at a caller function, and the failure it answers is total liveness loss, not statistical drift.

Apollo's loop runs *inside* the process it watches, on cheap in-band behavioral signals — turn latency, token counts, refusal rates — rather than event-loop lag or self-CPU, and its output is a typed object handed back to calling code, never a page to a human. The two loops are complementary by construction rather than by coincidence: Apollo's five channels are exactly the kind of soft, statistical drift (a slow refusal storm, an unbounded generation run) that a process wedged hard enough to need the fleet could never notice about itself in the first place — and a process healthy enough to run Apollo's EWMA baseline every turn is, by definition, not the failure mode the fleet exists to catch.
