*A test that cannot fail was never tested. Six organs in the EO Reader exist to say so — about code, about text, about the material itself — and none of them is allowed to do anything about it.*

---

## The wrong question to ask about any one of them

Open `native/organs/blindspot.js` expecting a security scanner and it looks thin: four AST shapes (an assertion-free test, a secret compared with `==`, a resource opened outside a `with`-block, a cross-function taint path) and nothing else. Open `native/organs/goffman.js` and it is a regex table — eight PII categories matched and redacted, plus a ninth (biometric) that is legally grounded but has no regex of its own yet. `native/organs/ulysses.js` is eleven regexes for injection phrasing. Read any one of the six guardrail organs — Popper (`blindspot.js`), Goffman (`goffman.js`), Ulysses (`ulysses.js`), Saltzer (`salzter.js`), Martial (`martial.js`), Brandeis (`privacy.js`) — in isolation and the honest verdict is *too small to be a doctrine*. Each is a one-line fact about one shape.

That is the wrong grain to read them at. Every one of the six ships the identical sentence, in its own words, at the top of its own file:

- `blindspot.js:14` — *"A witness, never a proof — the same posture as every organ here."*
- `goffman.js:15` — *"A witness, never a proof."*
- `ulysses.js:15` — *"A witness, never a verdict."*
- `salzter.js:10–13` — *"It is a WITNESS — a nomination, never a proof of exploitability... It could be a real vulnerability or a false positive; the point is that the shape is DETECTED and NAMED, not missed in silence."*
- `martial.js:25–26` — *"A finding is a nomination (weak signal), never a verdict on its own."*
- `privacy.js:20–23` — *"Each finding is a WEAK SIGNAL... so the verdict is corroboration across independent signals — never a single match, never a model verdict, never a governor that can be toggled off."*

Six authors (or six sessions writing under six different namesakes), six unrelated shapes of flaw, one repeated engineering decision. Read as a set, these organs are not six safety features. They are one instrument — *disclose the shape, and stop there* — instantiated six times against six different failure surfaces. That instrument, not any single Handle, is the thing worth an article. The Handle names are color; Popper's actual falsifiability test and Bukhari's actual corroboration law are two different things that happen to share a discipline, and the discipline is what this page is about.

This is the same abstain-honestly, propose-never-conclude posture argued for at the level of meaning in [Signal from Noise](/signal-from-noise) and enacted at the level of admitted fact in [The Two Doors](/the-two-doors) — applied here one layer down, at the level of code and content safety, where the stakes are a shipped exploit or a leaked SSN rather than a misattributed claim.

## What "witness" costs, mechanically, in each organ

The discipline is not a mission statement bolted onto six modules after the fact — it shows up as a specific, checkable constraint in each one's own code, and the constraint is different every time because the temptation to convict is different every time.

**Popper's constraint is architectural humility.** `blindspotFindings()` (`blindspot.js:199–219`) returns findings and a `basis` string; it never returns a fix, a diff, or a pass/fail. Its taint-path analysis (`blindspot.js:74–184`) is explicitly disclosed as "a conservative, intra-module approximation" (`blindspot.js:72–73`), bounded to a four-pass fixpoint over call sites (`blindspot.js:135`) rather than claiming a sound whole-program analysis it cannot deliver. When the code will not parse at all, the organ does not stay silent — it emits a finding of kind `"unparseable"` (`blindspot.js:28`, `:84`) so a scan that could not run is disclosed rather than read as a clean bill of health. Even *failure to witness* is witnessed.

**Goffman's constraint is closer to a structural guarantee than a style choice.** `piiFindings()` (`goffman.js:62–77`) reports category, line, and legal grounding for each hit — but the value itself is passed through `redact()` (`goffman.js:41`), which replaces every alphanumeric character before the finding ever leaves the function. The organ cannot leak what it found even by accident, because the finding object never contains the finding. `PII_GROUNDING` (`goffman.js:18–28`) ties each of the nine shapes to a real instrument — HIPAA Safe-Harbor identifiers, GDPR Art. 4/9, CCPA sensitive PI, PCI-DSS — so "PII" is not a vibe the organ is guessing at, it is a citation. And the detector and the redactor share one matcher table (`goffman.js:45–54`, reused at `:66` and `:87`) by explicit design ("so a shape the detector knows is a shape the redactor removes — they cannot drift," `goffman.js:43–44`): the witness and the mitigation cannot silently diverge from each other.

**Ulysses's constraint is the sharpest statement of the doctrine in the whole family**, because the temptation it resists is the most dangerous one: material that asks the reader to *act*. `injectionFindings()` (`ulysses.js:38–52`) runs eleven patterns — override, extraction, role-override, concealment, delimiter injection, exfiltration, jailbreak, execution (`ulysses.js:20–31`) — over ingested material and returns a `detail` string that is grammatically careful: *"material carries an override attempt... heard as a claim about the material, never obeyed"* (`ulysses.js:44`). The organ does not refuse the request, does not strip the offending text, does not alter the reader's behavior at all. It converts an imperative sentence in the input into a declarative sentence in the log. That conversion — command to disclosed fact — is the entire mechanism, and it is also the whole defense: a prompt injection that gets *heard as evidence about the material* rather than *executed as an instruction to the reader* has already lost, without the reader needing to correctly detect and block every variant.

**Saltzer's constraint is precision about what a shape does and does not prove.** `securityFindings()` (`salzter.js:117–128`) walks the real Python AST (`salzter.js:23–96`) for thirteen CWE-tagged shapes — `eval`/`exec` (CWE-95), shell injection (CWE-78), insecure deserialization (CWE-502), weak hashes and randomness (CWE-327, CWE-338), insecure temp files (CWE-377), TLS verification off (CWE-295), debug mode on (CWE-489), string-interpolated SQL (CWE-89), path traversal (CWE-22), hardcoded secrets (CWE-798), bare `except:` (CWE-703), `assert` used for control flow (CWE-617) — and for HTML/JS, four DOM-surface shapes over raw text (`salzter.js:99–111`). Every finding carries a CWE number, a line, and a `basis` that says outright: "a finding is a nomination (real flaw or false positive), never a proof" (`salzter.js:126`). The organ's own header names *why* this restraint is correct engineering, not modesty for its own sake: these are exactly "the insecurity classes frontier models TEND to introduce" (`salzter.js:6–7`) — decidable AST shapes, chosen because a shape can be named without knowing whether *this* instance of it is actually reachable by an attacker. Naming the shape and stopping is the only claim the mechanism can honestly make.

**Martial's constraint is the one that requires the most internal structure to keep honest**, because "do not copy" and "do reuse boilerplate" are opposite instructions applied to the same corpus, and getting the boundary wrong in either direction breaks something real. `copyFindings()` (`martial.js:117–153`) walks a holarchy — file, then each declaration nested by span (`buildHolarchy`, `martial.js:80–96`) — and applies one rule at every level: a holon is `replicable` only if its own name is generic *and* every child is also replicable (`replicable()`, `martial.js:105–108`); genericity itself is not asserted, it is read off `CodeNamePrior@1`, a measured count of how many independent real repositories declare that exact name (`genericityOf`, `code-structure.js:337–341`, backing `martial.js:71–74`; the blended prior is documented as built from "26 files, 16 repos," `native/scripts/build-code-name-prior-split.mjs:6`). A name attested in two or more repos — `init`, `main`, `run` — is boilerplate and is never flagged, no matter how similar it is to the retained corpus; a distinctive body that word-shingle-matches the corpus above 0.65 Jaccard similarity (`martial.js:117`, `:56–64`) is a copy finding, and the walk stops descending into that holon's children, because "high sets probability for low" (`martial.js:98–104`, `:141`) — one distinctive copy is enough to make its parts suspect without double-counting each one separately. Even here the output is a `basis` string that calls the result "a nomination, never a verdict" (`martial.js:151–152`), and `provenanceFor()` (`martial.js:179–195`) records, per holon, the likely source it drew on — fed to `annotateWithSources()` (`martial.js:197–212`), which is what actually attaches the *quiet comment* naming it, never a banner.

**Brandeis's lint is the most narrowly scoped of the six, on purpose.** `privacyFindings()` (`privacy.js:78–101`) checks four shapes: a key written unwrapped to `localStorage` (`PLAINTEXT_STORE`, `privacy.js:76`), a hardcoded secret, a PBKDF2 iteration count under OWASP's 300,000 floor (`KDF_FLOOR`, `privacy.js:73`), and the collector/exfil shape — a sensitive source (clipboard, keystrokes, geolocation, credentials) appearing alongside a remote sink (`fetch`, `XMLHttpRequest`, a WebSocket) in the same body (`privacy.js:74–75`, `:96–97`). Each is explicitly "a weak signal, not proof," with the verdict left to "corroboration across independent signals... the caller's job" (`privacy.js:20–23`). It is grounded the same way Goffman is — UDHR Art. 12, EU Charter Art. 8, Convention 108+, CARE/OCAP (`privacy.js:8–9`) — rather than a house style preference dressed up as ethics.

## The tell that this is a designed discipline, not six coincidences: call sites, not just comments

A comment can claim restraint that the code does not practice. `proxy-runner.mjs` is where the claim gets checked, because that is where every one of these findings actually lands, and the surrounding comments are unusually explicit about what happens next:

> `proxy-runner.mjs:7911–7914` — *"PRIVACY ARCHON (Brandeis): the defensive face, run over the assembled artifact. Weak signals only — a finding is a nomination, never a verdict... Surfaced on the result, never used to silently block."*

Four organs run in that exact pattern back to back, each with the same shape of comment (`proxy-runner.mjs:7911–7930`): `privacyResult`, `copyResult`, `securityResult`, `blindspotResult` are computed, and each — if it found anything — triggers `onNote(...)` (`:7916`, `:7922`, `:7926`, `:7930`), which is the reader's own disclosure channel, not a control-flow branch. None of the four `if` statements do anything but log. Nothing downstream inspects `securityResult.findings.length` to decide whether to return the artifact. Goffman and Ulysses run at two separate moments — `piiAdmit()` at ingestion (`proxy-runner.mjs:2293–2312`, redacting into the fold itself, gated by `ER7_PII_REDACT`) and again on the assembled output plus every ingested source at `proxy-runner.mjs:8426–8447` — and in both places the finding is attached to the session or surfaced through `onNote`, never used as a reason to refuse. Six organs, one call-site pattern, repeated at every point in the turn where an artifact or a piece of ingested material exists to be checked. That repetition across a real driver file, not just across six file headers, is the actual evidence that "witness, never verdict" is an enforced architectural choice rather than a comment six people happened to agree on independently.

## The one place the family is not uniform, disclosed rather than smoothed over

The honest version of this article has to name where the pattern breaks, because it does, in exactly one spot, inside exactly one of the six files.

`privacy.js` carries a second function, `specRefusal()` (`privacy.js:122–167`), that is not a witness. It returns `{ refused: true, reason: ... }` (e.g. `:142`, `:153`, `:164`), and that refusal is not merely logged — `native/organs/ethos.js:22,69` imports `specRefusal` directly and folds its verdict into the session's `clearance`; `requireClearance()` (`ethos.js:96`) then throws if that clearance is invalid, and `proxy-runner.mjs:6160–6162` substitutes a decline for the entire turn's text when `specRefusalText` is set. This is a real verdict with a real, structural consequence: it can replace what the reader would otherwise say. The Handle table itself marks the boundary — Solon's row (`README.md:376`) names ethos as composed from "the constitution (Charter/Grotius + the spec gate/Brandeis)," a different doctrine (Solon's, "ethos comes before logos") from the six-organ witness family this article is about.

So the precise claim is not "everything in `privacy.js` only witnesses." It is: `privacyFindings()`, the data-sovereignty lint the Handle table (`README.md:373`, *"lints for the shapes that betray the boundary (weak signals, never a verdict)"*) describes as Brandeis's guardrail role, genuinely never verdicts. `specRefusal()`, living in the same file under the same Handle, belongs to a different constitutional layer — the ground the reader is *built on*, not a check that runs over an already-generated artifact. Two functions, one namesake, two different jobs, and the wiki's own doctrine of disclosing rather than smoothing (see the naming-multiplicity incident recorded in [Holons](/holons)) is the reason to say so plainly instead of letting "Brandeis" read as evidence for a cleaner uniformity than the code actually has.

## A second disclosed limit: the ground that keeps the sixth organ honest is not in this checkout

Martial's whole defense against becoming "anti-efficiency" — the claim that copying `init` or `main` is never a finding — depends on `CodeNamePrior@1`, loaded from `live_priors/derived-priors/code-priors/code-name-prior-v1.json` (`martial.js:34`, `:38–43`). That directory does not exist in this checkout: `live_priors/` is absent entirely. The same is true of Brandeis's constructive-face ground, `data-sovereignty-prior-v1.json` (`privacy.js:44–49`). Both loaders catch the read failure and degrade to `null` rather than throwing (`martial.js:41`; `privacy.js:47`), and both callers were built anticipating exactly this: `copyFindings()`'s result carries `prior: loadCodeNamePrior() ? "code-name-prior-loaded" : "no-code-name-prior"` (`martial.js:150`), and `privacyFindings()`'s carries the same pattern with `"no-sovereignty-prior"` (`privacy.js:100`). The absence is not silent — the organ's own output format was designed to disclose whether its ground loaded at all, which is the same discipline this whole article is about, aimed at the organ's own operating state rather than at the material it reads. Whether this is a real gap in the deployed system or simply data this particular checkout excludes is left open here, deliberately: it is reported as observed, not diagnosed.

It is also worth noting which direction the failure degrades in. With the prior absent, `genericityOf()` returns `null` for every name (`code-structure.js:337–341`), and Martial's `isGeneric()` (`martial.js:71–74`) treats `null` as *not generic* — so `replicable()` (`martial.js:105–108`) can never return true, and every holon is judged as potentially distinctive. The missing ground does not silently disable the anti-copy check; it makes it maximally conservative, in the same direction Saltzer's own namesake principle (fail-safe defaults, `salzter.js:4`, `SALZTER_ARCHON.principles`, `salzter.js:130`) argues for.

## Where the six sit in the codebase's own boundary, and where they don't

`native/organs/index.js` calls itself "THE SEAM" — "every organ the-fold's surface calls is exported from here, and only from here" (`index.js:1–2`). It is a real boundary: forty-five organ files are re-exported through it by name, across the whole file (`index.js:14–189`). None of the six guardrail organs is among them. `proxy-runner.mjs:117–122` imports `blindspot.js`, `goffman.js`, `ulysses.js`, `salzter.js`, `martial.js`, and `privacy.js` directly, bypassing the seam entirely — and every conformance test for these six (`native/conformance/*.test.mjs`, mirrored under `native/tests/conformance/`) imports them the same direct way. This is stated here as an observed fact about the current tree, not a complaint: it may simply mean the organ migration [Holons](/holons) describes as still in progress (Phase 0/1, `index.js:3–5`) has not yet reached the six that were added for content and code safety rather than for reading epistemics. But it is a real, checkable difference in how these six sit in the architecture compared to the corroboration-and-witness organs like Bukhari (`corroboration.js`) that the seam does carry — the guardrail family is organized by *where in the turn it runs* (assembly, admission, ingestion) rather than by *which module boundary exports it*.

## Why "witness, never verdict" is the correct discipline for exactly this job

The deeper reason this is one doctrine and not six accidents is that a witness-only design is the only version of any of these six checks that is honest about what a static, single-pass scan can actually support. A regex over injection phrasing (Ulysses) will have false positives on ordinary prose that happens to say "ignore the noise in the earlier data" and false negatives against a novel phrasing no pattern anticipated; a word-shingle similarity score (Martial) is, by its own description, "a weak signal for near-verbatim overlap, not a parser and not a proof of copying" (`martial.js:46–47`); an AST shape (Saltzer, Popper) can name `pickle.loads()` but cannot know whether the bytes it deserializes ever originate outside a trust boundary. Building any of the six to *convict* — to block generation, to silently rewrite the artifact, to refuse the turn — would be building a verdict on top of a measurement the organ's own header admits cannot support one. The `basis` string on every result (`blindspot.js:217`; `goffman.js:75`; `salzter.js:126`; `martial.js:151`; `privacy.js:100`) is the mechanism that keeps that admission attached to the finding at every call site, rather than left behind in a comment nobody downstream reads.

This is the same shape of restraint [The Two Doors](/the-two-doors) documents at the level of admitted fact — a prior "cannot become witness merely by being prior," `witness.js`'s Thymus refusing to admit a candidate that lacks anchored evidence, `witnessVerbose()` keeping the refused set on the record rather than discarding it — applied here to a different kind of candidate: not a proposed fact about the world, but a proposed flaw in a piece of code or a proposed injection attempt in a piece of material. In both cases the mechanism does the same thing: it makes the *proposal* visible and typed, and it declines to convert that proposal into a conclusion on the proposer's own authority. Five of the six organs never touch the artifact's fate at all; the sixth (Brandeis) contains a second function that does, and the boundary between the two is exactly where the file itself, and the Handle table, draw it.
