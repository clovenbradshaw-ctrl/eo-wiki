*Nine operators, but only three shapes of reasoning underneath them. Read the cube's domain axis correctly and you are not looking at nine miscellaneous verbs — you are looking at arithmetic, geometry, and calculus, each contributing exactly three primitives, each with its own way of failing.*

## The claim, and where it actually lives

The cube's domain axis has always had three named values — Existence, Structure, Interpretation (`native/kernel/cube.js:13`, `DOMAINS = ["Existence", "Structure", "Interpretation"]`). What `native/docs/THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md` adds, worked out in conversation on 2026-09-01 and marked at nomination standing rather than sealed law, is a reading of what those three domains *are*: not three arbitrary buckets but the three classical mathematics, each supplying its domain's three operators as that mathematics' own primitives (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:15-27`):

| domain | mathematics | its three operators |
|---|---|---|
| Existence | arithmetic | NUL (zero), SIG (the mark), INS (the successor) |
| Structure | geometry | SEG (the cut), CON (incidence), SYN (construction) |
| Interpretation | calculus | DEF (the bound), EVA (comparison to a bound), REC (the discontinuity) |

This is checkable against code, not just prose. `cube.js`'s `OP_DOMAIN` table (`native/kernel/cube.js:28-38`) assigns exactly `{NUL, SIG, INS}` to Existence, `{SEG, CON, SYN}` to Structure, and `{DEF, EVA, REC}` to Interpretation — the same grouping the document proposes, independently confirmable by reading the table rather than trusting the essay. The document is explicit about which direction authority runs: "Nothing here changes any table; if the tables move, this document follows or falls" (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:329,342-343`). That is the right relationship for a reading to have to its ground — the interpretation is answerable to the code, not the other way around — and it is the discipline the document's own closing note names outright: "a code table is a giver for code, never for canon" (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:357`) — the same caution [Holons](/holons) practices when it treats a fully-populated cube-cell registry as a coverage measure, never proof of behavior, on its own terms.

There is a small, satisfying structural note the document points at and doesn't oversell: arithmetic's domain *opens* on a zero (NUL) and calculus's domain *closes* on a re-zeroing (REC) — "the empty count and the reset accumulation, two zeros at the two ends" (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:26-29`). It is decoration, not evidence, and the document treats it that way.

## Each mathematics, and where it breaks — not just where it works

The part of the document worth taking seriously is not that each domain resembles a mathematics in the abstract, but that each mathematics is given an actual **wall** — a named failure mode, backed by a real incident in the codebase — which is exactly the discipline [Nine Instructions](/nine-instructions) asks of the whole framework: don't just claim a structure works, say what would break it, and disclose when it already has.

**Arithmetic's wall: it presupposes the unit.** Counting is meaningless until individuation has already settled what counts as one thing (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:31-40`). The document names two real, disclosed failures at this wall rather than a hypothetical: "junk recurred identically to real anchors (frequency counts tokens, not beings)," and `witnesses.length` "double-counted one source in two costumes until `distinctSources` corrected it" (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:33-42`). Both read as post-mortems, not boasts — a negative finding stated as plainly as a positive one. The document's summary judgment is blunt: "Every arithmetic failure measured this week was an Existence failure upstream" (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:42-43`) — arithmetic doesn't fail on its own terms, it fails when the domain underneath it (Existence — what counts as a *thing*) was never settled.

**Geometry's wall: position is not identity.** Everything meaningful gets coordinates (`ref#start–end`), coordinate spaces are never silently mixed, and identity travels by *containment* rather than by shape (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:45-58`) — the same fold rule, the document notes, that "unified prefix/suffix/infix and works unchanged for a Japanese substring or a motif's time-span." The wall itself is concrete: "a sentence-initial capital is position; East and West Cliff are adjacent and different; co-presence raises the bar, never the score" (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:54-58`). Geometry locates. It does not, by itself, get to name.

**Calculus's three walls.** Calculus is read as event-sourced — "never store the state, store the flow," with every `fold*` function read as an integration (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:59-66`). Three named failure modes go with it: the **dark room** (optimizing your own surprise-derivative toward zero just confirms what you already believed — "built by accident this week, now structurally guarded twice"); **uncalibrated rates** (an invented likelihood ratio "is a change of units that fails invisibly"); and **integration that isn't idempotent over identity** (a re-sighting of the same event must be a no-op, "so the integral never double-counts one event") (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:66-81`). That last one names its own dependency explicitly: "calculus depending on arithmetic depending on Existence" — the three mathematics are not independent; a failure at the arithmetic floor propagates upward.

## The mode-triad: a second, orthogonal three

Here is the part of the document that actually earns the word "cube" rather than just "table," and it is the reason there are nine operators rather than some other number.

Cross-cutting the three domains is a second triad — not a subject-matter triad but an *operation-type* triad, the same in every domain: Differentiate, Relate, Generate. This is not asserted; it's read straight off `cube.js`'s own `OP_MODE` table (`native/kernel/cube.js:16-26`):

```
NUL: Differentiate   SIG: Relate   INS: Generate     (Existence / arithmetic)
SEG: Differentiate   CON: Relate   SYN: Generate     (Structure / geometry)
DEF: Differentiate   EVA: Relate   REC: Generate     (Interpretation / calculus)
```

The document's own history with this table is worth keeping, because the correction is more informative than a clean derivation would have been: an earlier draft of the document said "the same triad appears twice" — treating the mode axis as a repeat of the domain axis rather than a genuinely different triad of operation-types each mathematics carries internally — and a same-day re-check caught the conflation and restated the mode-triad as its own thing, verified cell-by-cell against `cube.js`'s `OP_MODE` table (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:123-134`). The corrected reading names calculus's own mode-triad "differentiate / equate / integrate" — DEF as differentiate (a definition as an unproven bound), EVA as equate (comparing an accumulation to that bound), REC as integrate (the reset) — and that phrase is not decoration, since "the mode is literally named after the calculus pair" (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:138-139`). Arithmetic's mode-triad reads as subtract/equate/add; geometry's as cut/connect/construct.

This is worth a flag: at least one *other* wiki article's table of the same cells — [The EO capacity ground](/the-eo-phase-space-cube)'s Mode×Domain table — currently prints Differentiating = {NUL, SEG, EVA}, disagreeing with `cube.js`'s actual `OP_MODE` on exactly the cell (EVA vs. DEF) that this section's own history shows how easy it is to get wrong even when checking against the code directly. Checked directly against `native/kernel/cube.js:16-26` in the current tree, `cube.js` says DEF is Differentiate and EVA is Relate — matching THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md's table, not the phase-space-cube article's. This is stated here as a discrepancy between two wiki articles against the same code, not adjudicated further; a reader who needs the ground truth should read the table, not either essay.

The payoff of the two triads is a real explanation, not a coincidence, for the operator count: **9 = 3 subjects (which mathematics) × 3 shared operation-types (the mode).** The document is careful not to oversell the correspondence between the two triads as canonical — "whether the two triads correspond canonically... is genuinely open" — and reports one candidate pairing (NUL / CON / REC as each mathematics' arguably most characteristic operation: zero, incidence, re-integration) explicitly as "an observation, not a theorem" (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:141-148`).

## The stance face is where the mathematics claim becomes testable

Section VI of the document reframes the cube's three 3×3 projections — act face (domain × mode), terrain face (domain × grain), stance face (mode × grain) — in a way that matters for what comes next: each face collapses one axis and stacks the collapsed axis as a hidden three-element fiber. On the **stance face**, the collapsed axis is domain — so *every stance cell is a three-story stack*: the same posture instantiated once in arithmetic, once in geometry, once in calculus (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:153-183`). That makes the stance face **the isomorphism face**: a stance name is well-defined exactly to the degree that the three mathematics genuinely share that operation-shape. *Binding* (Relate·Figure) is named as the column that "went full first" — instantiated as counting, as connecting, and as accumulating (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:186-190`).

The document draws a careful distinction here that is easy to blur: a stance being "FULL" (every cell occupied) is a proven *instantiation* in all three mathematics, never on its own a proven *isomorphism* — "coverage shows an organ exists per story of the stack, never that the three organs share structure" (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:182-188`). The isomorphism is a hypothesis a full stance makes testable. That hypothesis is what section VIII goes and tests.

## Two pre-registered predictions, both measured, both checkable now

This is the strongest part of the document, and the part worth verifying rather than repeating on faith — so it was verified directly against the actual files, not taken from the doc's own summary.

**Prediction 1: a full stance's organs should port across mathematics with only the adapter changing, core untouched.** The document is explicit that its first attempt at a test case was wrong — it originally cited `contest.js` as precedent, but contest transfers across *media*, and medium isn't one of the cube's three axes at all; "no domain-transfer has yet been demonstrated" was the honest status before the real trial (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:264-271`).

The real trial: `native/organs/binding-core.js` extracts Binding (Relate·Figure) as one algebra — a figure seeking its counterpart in a field, scored against a declared criterion, demanding a unique clearing winner (a tie refuses as ambiguous, never a coin flip), with an optional foil probe that refuses a criterion clearing a designated decoy, and every outcome carrying its evidence (`binding-core.js:1-41`). The file contains zero domain vocabulary by mechanical assertion: `binding-transfer.test.mjs`'s first test reads the core's own source and asserts that words like "mention," "referent," "edge," "claim," "verdict," and "witness" never appear in it (`binding-transfer.test.mjs:30-35`). Three thin adapters were then built on top of that one core and checked for agreement with the three *real* organs on real material:

- an arithmetic adapter (mention → referent) reproducing the real `makeReferentIndex`'s resolution on an unambiguous fragment and its refusal on a shared one (`binding-transfer.test.mjs:38-83`);
- a geometry adapter (claim → edge) reproducing the real relation reader's bound and not-bound verdicts on the same sentence pair (`binding-transfer.test.mjs:86-117`);
- a calculus adapter (testimony → verdict) reproducing the armed-select protocol's states / foiled / no-testimony outcomes (`binding-transfer.test.mjs:120-149`).

Five tests are defined for this trial — the wall test plus the three domain-agreement tests plus a final structural check that the three adapters share one imported `bind()` and never monkey-patch it (`binding-transfer.test.mjs:151-160`). All five are gated on a sibling `the-fold` checkout being present alongside eoreader7 (the source of the real `makeReferentIndex`/`makeRelationReader` organs each adapter is checked against): with that sibling present, the suite reports 5/5 passing; run against an eoreader7 checkout alone, the suite's own guard (`resolveFoldSibling`, in `native/eval/the-fold/lib/fold-sibling.mjs`) makes all five tests skip rather than fail. So "5/5" describes the paired-checkout result the trial was actually run under, not something an isolated eoreader7 checkout reproduces on its own — a scope note worth carrying alongside the result, not a reason to doubt it. The document's own report — "Zero core edits between domains — the one mid-build fix was adapter content" (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:280-292`) — holds up against the code regardless: the only mid-trial correction recorded is that the referent field held IDs rather than display strings, so the adapter's own `score` function had to read each candidate's face through the index's `represent()` call — an adapter-side fact, not a core edit (`binding-transfer.test.mjs:45-52`). The document is honest about scope, too: this is "agreement on specimens, not behavioral equivalence" — the real organs carry corroboration, spans, and regime tracking the extracted core does not; what transferred is the decision *shape*, not the full organ (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:284-292`).

**Prediction 2: a stance empty in one domain but occupied in the others should be derivable, sight unseen, from its siblings.** DEF·Ground (Clearing at Atmosphere — declaring the ground a judgment gets rendered against) was empty. The design was committed to `native/docs/def-ground-derivation.md` *before* the module existed — the transposition of NUL·Ground's declaration gate (`measure.js`'s `admit`, which refuses an undeclared draw count, window, or seed) and SEG·Ground's extent-and-units gate into the calculus column, predicting `declareFrame` with typed `undeclared_frame` refusals, frame IDs stamped on verdicts, and a `cross_frame` comparison wall (`def-ground-derivation.md:24-47`; `THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:294-312`).

`native/organs/frame.js` — Handle: Alhazen, after Ibn al-Haytham's dictum to declare the frame before comparing results — shipped exactly that: `declareFrame({organs, givers, numbers})` refuses, typed, on any unnamed organ, absent giver, or unvalued number (`frame.js:34-38,61-69`); `framed()` stamps a verdict with its frame's content-addressed id (`frame.js:77`); and `comparable()` refuses cross-frame comparisons, naming the components that differ rather than letting the difference read as a fact about the material (`frame.js:87-102`). The pre-registration's own gate was a real, already-measured specimen: one suite (`hypergraph.test.mjs`) had scored 54/58 under one provider frame and 52/58 under another, invisible for as long as the frame went undeclared — comparing those two numbers without declaring the frames is exactly the sin the wall types (`frame.js:24-29`; `def-ground-derivation.md:49-60`). The document reports "zero departures" from the pre-registered design (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:312-315`), and reading `frame.js` against `def-ground-derivation.md` line by line confirms it: every predicted function name, refusal type, and behavior in the derivation appears in the shipped module. The document does not let one success stand in for a law: "one trial is one point, not a law" (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:318`), and it names exactly where the next trial is blocked (the remaining Ground-row cells, CON and INS, gated on another session's boundary).

## The canonical operator chain is math-major, and a flagged discrepancy has since closed

The document's account of the canonical operator sequence is worth including because its own history demonstrates the discipline it argues for. Section VII was wrong twice in opposite directions before landing: a first draft asserted the domain-major chain without checking; a re-check against `task-log.js`'s `OPERATOR_ORDER` then over-corrected, demoting the domain-major chain as superseded by a divergent engine constant — itself an inversion. A second check, against the actual canon sources (`eoreader6/CUBE.md` line 39; the handbook's construction-language chapter, which reports that "of nearly thirteen hundred possible orderings, only this one survives basic consistency checks"; the eoreader4.1 lineage), restored the domain-major reading: **NUL → SIG → INS → SEG → CON → SYN → DEF → EVA → REC** — arithmetic's three operators, then geometry's, then calculus's (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:206-233`). (Neither `eoreader6/CUBE.md` nor the handbook chapter cited here is present in the eoreader7 checkout used for this check; that citation chain is reported as THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md's own sourcing, not independently re-verified against those external files. The order itself, independently, is corroborated by [The Nine Operators](/the-nine-operators)'s own statement of the same helix, and by `cube.js`'s own derived `OPERATOR_CHAIN`, below.)

At the time the document was written, it flagged a live discrepancy rather than hiding it: `task-log.js` in eoreader7's kernel carried a literal `OPERATOR_ORDER` constant that interleaved the domains, differing from the canonical chain (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:239-249`). Checked against the current tree, that discrepancy has since closed: `native/kernel/task-log.js:9` now reads `export const OPERATOR_ORDER = OPERATOR_CHAIN;`, deriving the order from `cube.js`'s own tables rather than restating it by hand. `cube.js`'s own header on `OPERATOR_CHAIN` names the fix directly: the hand-written order "had drifted from the very tables it was restating," an audit found no test on disk or in the native suite that could tell the two orderings apart, and "the burden was on the divergence, and it was not met" (`native/kernel/cube.js:68-93`). This is disclosed here as a limit finding worth stating plainly: the source document's own flagged gap is not a permanent feature of the codebase, and a reader relying only on the document without re-checking the current tree would report a discrepancy that no longer exists.

## What this is not: the phase-space cube's numeric axis

It matters to keep this reading separate from a different, more speculative layer that lives one door down in the wiki. [The EO capacity ground](/the-eo-phase-space-cube) proposes a numeric/transcendental coordinate system on top of the *Object* axis (Ground/Figure/Pattern) — Condition = 2, Particular = √2, Regularity = 2^√2, argued through the Gelfond–Schneider theorem, with a "mathematical gradient" counted in irrational and transcendental thresholds crossed per axis. That article's own editorial note is unambiguous about its status: `cube.js`'s `GRAINS` names the same three positions as plain strings ("Ground", "Figure", "Pattern"), "with no numeric coordinates anywhere in the module," and the numeric apparatus should be read as "a speculative layer on top of the implemented Ground/Figure/Pattern trichotomy, not as the axis's canonical or implemented naming."

THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md is a different kind of claim about a different axis entirely — it reads the *domain* axis (Existence/Structure/Interpretation) as literally three named branches of mathematics, each contributing a real, currently-shipped operator triad, checked directly against `OP_DOMAIN` and `OP_MODE`, and backed by two measured, pre-registered trials rather than a numerological gradient. Where the phase-space-cube article's transcendental-number layer has no counterpart anywhere in the kernel, THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md's domain-as-mathematics reading recovers a table that already exists in `cube.js` and adds a falsifiable claim on top of it — the stance-face isomorphism hypothesis — that has already been tested twice, once in the affirmative on a full stance and once in the affirmative on a from-scratch derivation of an empty one. The two documents share a fondness for classical mathematics as a naming device; only one of them grounds that naming in code that runs and tests that have executed.

## Standing

The document names its own status precisely: nomination, not sealed law, checkable against `cube.js`, `TERRAIN_BY_DOMAIN`, and `STANCE_BY_MODE`, and explicitly subordinate to those tables if they ever move (`THE-THREE-MATHEMATICS.md:329-343`). What raises it above a merely clever reading is that it did the thing [Nine Instructions](/nine-instructions) asks any closure claim to do: it wrote down what would falsify it before running the test, ran the test, and reported the result whichever way it landed — including the corrections to its own earlier drafts, kept rather than quietly rewritten out.
