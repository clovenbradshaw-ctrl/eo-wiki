# Identity and Definition: One Fold, Two Ends

**Record ID:** wiki:identity-and-definition  
**DB ID:** 81  
**Tags:** 301

**Status:** published

---

**Identity** is what a thing is for someone, not what it is in itself. Take everything the reading connects to a thing (what it touches, what happens around it, how that changes over time) and cut it off where the difference stops mattering. That cut-out neighborhood, seen from a particular perspective, is its identity. Two names are the same thing when their neighborhoods match under that cut, and when swapping one out for a generic stand-in disturbs the surroundings in the same way, by more than chance would. That is why identity is relative (it depends on the for-whom), revisable (more reading can move it), and earned (the thing has to carry weight, so idle things never count as the same).

**A definition** is that same fold written down in a portable phrase, by someone, so it can be carried and reused. "Faction" on its own is just a word, and Madison's sentence compresses what he wants it to mean into something that can travel. Because it is compressed, the phrase can't replace the word, which is why my substitution test failed. What it does is shift the odds: once a word is defined, some later uses become much more likely and others much less. It governs by changing probabilities, not by laying down a rule. The giver is the for-whom, so a definition is only as authoritative as its giver. HUD's definition of affordability governs HUD's usage, and a planning document borrowing it may use the word differently.

So the two are one thing seen from two ends. Identity is the fold found by reading, and a definition is the fold stated in advance. A definition succeeds when what you find matches what was stated, and it drifts when the reading moves away from the stated fold.

Three things are not established. The identity definition is still a nomination in the code, with its measurements pending. The claim that a definition is a prior on usage is untested, and the Federalist result is only suggestive support. And I have not yet shown that a definition predicts anything beyond its own words, which is the control I proposed.
