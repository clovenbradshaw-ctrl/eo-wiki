SIG (○, σ) is the second operator in Experiential Ontology's dependency helix. It occupies the position Relating × Existence — the figure role within the Existence triad. Its glyph is ○, the open circle — attention directed, not yet filled. Its Greek letter is σ (sigma), derived from σημεῖον (*sēmeion*) — the mark, the point, the place where something registered. The code SIG is not an abbreviation. It is a connotation-rich signifier — signal, sign, significance, sigil, signature — that distributes across Ground, Figure, and Pattern without collapsing to any single Object position.

SIG is the capacity for appearing. Not for naming, not for partitioning, not for interpreting — those are downstream operations that presuppose SIG and build on it. SIG is simpler and more primitive: something shows up for a system. A chemoreceptor on the surface of an *Escherichia coli* responds to a sugar molecule. The molecule *appears* to the bacterium's navigational system. Before the receptor fires, the molecule is physically present but not present-for-the-bacterium. After, it registers. Nothing was partitioned. Nothing was named. Something appeared.

A system that has SIG available can have things show up for it. A system that has lost SIG is anesthetic — undifferentiated with respect to its environment, incapable of registering anything as present. The degradation of SIG is not the loss of a vocabulary or a boundary. It is the loss of the capacity for anything to matter.

SIG occupies Mode = 1 (Relating), Domain = −1 (Existence).

Mode 1 is the binding operation, the identity element of multiplication. "Relating holds without distorting." Mode 0 (Differentiating) cuts. Mode 2 (Generating) produces. Mode 1 preserves what it touches.

Domain −1 is Existence, the most contracted pole. "Is anything here at all?"

Compare the Existence triad:

1. **NUL** = Differentiating (0) × Existence (−1). The *withdrawal* from the existence question. Recognizing that nothing is there.
2. **SIG** = Relating (1) × Existence (−1). The *engagement* with the existence question. Something is there-for-a-system. Holding existence without distorting it.
3. **INS** = Generating (2) × Existence (−1). The *production* within the existence question. Something comes into being with enduring identity.

The coordinates predict an operator that engages with existence without cutting it (that would be Differentiating) and without producing within it (that would be Generating). The transformation is relational: something becomes present-to-a-system. SIG is the appearing of what is, not the making of what wasn't.

The Mode alignment is decisive. The old name "Distinction" is a Differentiating act — it cuts this-from-that. But SIG sits at Mode = 1 (Relating), not Mode = 0 (Differentiating). The coordinates were always pointing at something relational: not cutting, but registering. Not partitioning, but showing up. The tension between "distinction" and the Relating coordinate was structural evidence that the name was wrong.

## Biological grounding

SIG's biological instantiation is the chemoreceptor that responds to a molecule. In the reference organism (*Escherichia coli* navigating a chemical gradient), SIG is the receptor firing — the moment the molecule binds and triggers a conformational change in the receptor protein.

Under the appearing reading, the biology is cleaner than it was under "distinction." The chemoreceptor does not partition the world into sugar-and-not-sugar. It does not draw a boundary. The molecule *appears to the bacterium's navigational system*. Before the receptor fires, the molecule exists (it is physically present in the medium) but it has not appeared — it is not there-for-the-bacterium. After the receptor fires, the molecule is present-to-the-system. The bacterium can now respond to it. Nothing was partitioned. Nothing was named. Something showed up.

The triad at the biological level:

1. **NUL:** The receptor that can respond and doesn't. The molecule is there but does not appear. The non-event that shapes downstream behavior because the machinery was present and inactive.
2. **SIG:** The receptor that responds. The molecule appears. Something is now there-for-the-system.
3. **INS:** The downstream creation of a persistent state — a chemotactic memory, a behavioral change, a flagellar motor adjustment. Something comes into being as a consequence of the appearing.

A rock in the same chemical soup does not have SIG. A rock is inert — nothing appears to it because it has no machinery for appearing. The bacterium has SIG because it has receptors capable of being affected by environmental molecules. The capacity for appearing is the capacity for being a system to which things can show up.

This grounds the universality claim. SIG operates at the level of a single cell. No brain, no language, no culture, no consciousness. If appearing works here — if the capacity for something to register is biologically real and consequential at the cellular level — then SIG describes what transformation is at its most primitive non-trivial level: the first operation that all subsequent operations presuppose.

## The naming history

The operator at position two has been named four times. Each name was correct about something and wrong about something else. The sequence is not a record of confusion but of progressive refinement under the pressure of the biological grounding test.

### Designation (2024 – February 2026)

The first formal appearance of "DES — Designate" is in the Change Operator Manual. The manual defined DES with practitioner-oriented precision: *"An existing thing receives or loses an identity designation. It becomes (or ceases to be) a recognized kind of thing."* Emission rules were specified: emit DES when a type is assigned, when a schema is applied to untyped data, when a classification is added or removed.

"Designation" worked well at the institutional and computational scale. Naming a column. Setting a property type. Declaring "Revenue (GAAP)" versus "Revenue (Internal)." Tagging an item as "Team" versus "Department." The dependency argument for the helix ordering mapped DES to DDL expressions — `CREATE TABLE`, type constraints, `CHECK` constraints — and the alignment was natural.

The name broke when the framework was grounded biologically. Designation implies a designator — a conscious agent who chooses a label. But the operator occupies helix position 2, after NUL and before INS, which requires it to be more primitive than naming. A bacterium does not designate a sugar molecule. Designation captures only the top of the operator's range.

**What Designation got right:** The institutional and data-systems instantiation. Type-assignment, schema definition, and category application are real SIG applications at the computational scale.

**What Designation got wrong:** It presupposed a conscious agent at a position that must work pre-linguistically and pre-consciously. It inflated the operator by loading schema, typing, and DDL onto a single position — work that the EO///DB manual later redistributed to INS (entity creation) and DEF (establishing what holds).

### Distinction (February 2026)

The rename was driven by the biological grounding test. Each operator must be instantiable at the level of *E. coli* navigating a chemical gradient. "Designation" failed the test; "Distinction" passed it. The rename drew on Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form* (1969): the primitive act of drawing a distinction. This worked at every scale — from molecular receptor response to philosophical category creation.

**What Distinction got right:** The pre-linguistic, pre-conscious level. A chemoreceptor distinguishes a molecule from its medium. The operation is more primitive than naming.

**What Distinction got wrong:** It encoded a structural assumption: *partition*. Spencer-Brown's "draw a distinction" produces "this" and "not-this" — a boundary operation. But boundary-drawing is the job of SEG (Differentiating × Structure), the fourth operator in the helix. This created a long-standing overlap between positions two and four that required explanation: SIG draws *conceptual* distinctions, SEG draws *structural* boundaries. The explanation was always somewhat strained, because the operations share the same underlying logic — cutting one thing from another. More fundamentally, "distinction" is a Differentiating act (Mode 0), but SIG sits at Mode 1 (Relating). The name was fighting its own coordinates.

### SIG (March 2026)

The code SIG was adopted because its connotation cloud distributes across the Object axis without privileging any single position:

1. **Signal** (Ground): something propagates, is detectable — the physical event of mattering. A receptor fires. A sensor trips. A threshold is crossed.
2. **Sign** (Figure): something stands for something else — the relational event of mattering. A mark on a page. A symptom in a body. A flag in a dataset.
3. **Significance** (Pattern): something matters within a frame — the interpretive event of mattering. The mark is important. The symptom is diagnostic. The flag demands attention.

The secondary resonances — sigil, signature, sigma — reinforce without collapsing. The key property: "signature" is more primitive than the Ground/Figure/Pattern trichotomy. The mark that something has registered — before you know whether what registered is a condition, an entity, or a regularity. The code does the thing it names: it gestures toward a position in the capacity ground rather than collapsing to a single face.

**What SIG-as-code got right:** The connotation cloud. The refusal to fix a single expansion. The dissolution of the SIG/SEG overlap (SIG = something registers; SEG = something gets bounded).

**What SIG-as-code left unresolved:** The conceptual content. "Signal," "significance," and "salience" were all discussed as possible expansions; the decision not to fix one was correct procedurally but left the ontological question open. What *is* the operation, if not distinction?

### Appearing (May 2026)

The answer came from three convergent sources that arrived independently.

**The empirical exemplars.** The cross-linguistic study's top SIG-classified clauses, drawn from 19,764 annotated clauses across 41 languages, showed a consistent semantic pattern: observational present-tense registration of what is or isn't present. "Pizza is available there." "The flowers arrived at exactly the right time." "Someone finally came." "I was amazed at how quickly the food arrived." These are not distinctions being drawn. They are reports of *appearing* — something showing up for an experiencing subject.

**The coordinate semantics.** Relating (1) × Existence (−1) predicts an operator that *engages with existence without cutting it*. The Relating mode preserves what it touches. Applied to the Existence domain, that means: something becomes present-to-a-system without the system changing, partitioning, or producing what becomes present. That is appearing. The tension between "distinction" and the Relating coordinate dissolved: appearing is constitutively relational (something appears *to* something), not constitutively differentiating (cutting this from that).

**The cross-traditional convergence.** Ten philosophical and scientific traditions, working independently across two millennia, have isolated an operator that does the work EO assigns to SIG — a registering-as-present that precedes naming, partitioning, and categorization. The convergence is too strong to be coincidental (see §Cross-traditional grounding below).

**What Appearing gets right:** It matches the coordinates (Relating, not Differentiating). It matches the exemplars (registration of presence, not partition). It matches the biological grounding (the molecule appears to the bacterium, it does not get partitioned by the bacterium). It dissolves the SIG/SEG overlap completely: SIG is appearing, SEG is partitioning; no overlap remains. And it aligns with the Given-Log's own epistemic status: "Every entry in the Given-Log is a phenomenon — an appearance of something to an observer operating within a particular frame."

**What Appearing risks:** The word carries phenomenological connotations that may overweight the conscious-experience end of the operator's range. A chemoreceptor is not having a phenomenological experience. The defense: appearing is used here in the pre-phenomenological sense — φαίνεσθαι (*phainesthai*), to show itself, the root of "phenomenon." The phenomenon is the appearance, not the experience of the appearance. A molecule showing up for a chemoreceptor is a phenomenon in this sense.

### What survived every rename

Across all four names — and across the pre-naming phase where the position had domain-specific labels but no shared operator name — the following have not changed:

**Position in the capacity ground.** Relating × Existence. This coordinate was fixed before the operator had a name.

**Position in the helix.** Second. After NUL, before INS. The dependency chain NUL → [position two] → INS is established by exhaustive computational verification across all 1,296 admissible orderings.

**Formal specification.** The relational algebra mapping and the proof's Definition 2.2 do not depend on the operator's English name.

**Greek notation.** σ (sigma).

**Practitioner glyph.** ○ (open circle).

## SIG is not SEG

The single most consequential structural claim in this article is that appearing (SIG) and partitioning (SEG) are different operations at different positions in the helix. This was always asserted but difficult to defend cleanly under the "distinction" name, because distinction is inherently a partition-adjacent concept. Under "appearing," the separation is clean.

**By coordinates:** SIG = Relating (1) × Existence (−1). SEG = Differentiating (0) × Structure (+1). Different Mode, different Domain. They share no axis value.

**By biology:** The chemoreceptor registers a molecule (SIG). The cell membrane separates inside from outside (SEG). The tick has three *Merkmale* — butyric acid, warmth, hairlessness — each of which *appears* to the tick's sensory system. The tick does not "partition the world into butyric-acid and not-butyric-acid." There is no antecedent undifferentiated field from which the molecule gets carved out. The molecule binds the receptor; the cue *is* the appearing. Spencer-Brown's "draw a distinction" presupposes a markable indistinction; SIG presupposes no such field.

**By the Trika tattva hierarchy:** In Kashmir Shaivism's 36-tattva cosmology, partition (*māyā-tattva*, tattva 6) is formally placed downstream of appearing (*Śiva-Śakti-tattvas*, 1-2). This is independent ancient warrant for the EO claim. The traditions did not copy each other. The structural ordering converges.

**By composition:** Under "Distinction," SIG(SEG(x)) meant "distinguish a boundary" — nearly redundant. Under "Appearing," the same nesting becomes "register a boundary" — notice that a partition exists. SIG(EVA(x)) shifts from "distinguish a state change" to "register a state change." CON(SIG(x)) becomes "connect what has appeared" — the bacterium's receptor-to-motor link. The compositions are more informative and less redundant.

The vocabularies are not interchangeable. *Vivarta* implies a substratum; *prajñapti* refuses one. *Prakāśa* doubles with *vimarśa* (reflexive self-touch); *causal efficacy* is vector-structured without reflexivity. But eight features are cross-traditional:

### The Advaita connection

The deepest structural parallel is with Śaṅkara's Advaita Vedānta, which distinguishes three levels of reality — not two:

1. **Pāramārthika** (the absolute). Nirguṇa Brahman. Without qualities, without predication. NUL.
2. **Vyāvahārika** (the empirical/conventional). The world as it appears to experiencing subjects. Not the world-as-created, not the world-in-itself. The world *as it shows up*. SIG's domain.
3. **Prātibhāsika** (the illusory). Dream, error, hallucination. The failure mode of SIG.

The middle term is the key. Vyāvahārika is not NUL (the absolute), not INS (the created world), and not a failure. It is the domain of appearing-as-such. Māyā, in Śaṅkara's technical usage, is the *śakti* (power, capacity) through which the undifferentiated appears as differentiated — not "illusion" (the common Western mistranslation) but the *capacity for appearing*. Māyā-śakti is SIG as a capacity, not as an event.

The Advaitic doctrine of *vivartavāda* (apparent transformation, as opposed to *pariṇāmavāda*, real transformation) gives a precise formal property: the NUL ground does not *become* the differentiated world (that would be INS). It *appears as* the differentiated world via SIG. The rope does not become a snake. The rope appears as a snake. The appearing is the transformation. SIG is the vivarta operator.

The doctrine of *adhyāsa* (superimposition) maps the SIG → DEF boundary. SIG makes something appear. DEF establishes what it means. Adhyāsa is what happens when DEF gets applied to a SIG-appearance without tracking that the meaning was assigned, not intrinsic. The rope appears (SIG); "snake" is superimposed (DEF without provenance). The error is not in the appearing. It is in the untracked DEF.

A historical caution: the strict equation "vivarta = Śaṅkara" is a post-Padmapāda systematization. Śaṅkara's own preferred terms are *adhyāsa*, *avidyā*, and *nāma-rūpa*. The heavy substantivization of māyā as a *jaḍātmikā śakti* is Padmapāda's innovation, defended by Prakāśātman. EO's citation practice should attribute the strict vivartavāda formulation to Prakāśātman, not Śaṅkara.

### Whitehead's prehension

Alfred North Whitehead's *Symbolism* (1927) supplies the cleanest single Western analog: *causal efficacy* as the primitive perceptual mode. It is "vague, haunting, unmanageable" — the heavy, primitive feeling of being-affected that dominates the experience of organisms that have a sense for the fate from which they emerged and the fate toward which they go. It is pre-conceptual, pre-partitional, vector-structured (past→present), and biologically universal.

Whitehead's polemic against Hume — that presentational immediacy (the clear, distinct "red, there") is not primary but a late, superficial product of complexity — is the argument SIG needs. The technically precise layer is not the articulate perception but the vague feeling of being-affected. The technical structure of a *prehension* (subject, datum, subjective form) gives EO a non-set-theoretic formal vocabulary: SIG is a vector with three components — registering-system, registered-content, and how-of-being-affected — not a set-membership operation.

### The Sellars constraint

The most serious philosophical objection to treating appearing as ontologically primitive is Wilfrid Sellars's "Myth of the Given" (1956). Sellars demonstrates that nothing can simultaneously be (a) non-inferentially known, (b) propositionally articulable to serve as evidence, and (c) knowable without prior concept-possession. If SIG were advanced as the evidential foundation of justified belief, the objection would be fatal.

EO's defense is to concede the Sellarsian point in its proper domain and show that domain is narrower than the strong reading suggests. SIG is ontologically primitive but *not epistemically foundational*. SIG does not justify; it registers. Justification belongs to the downstream operators DEF and EVA, which operate in the space of reasons. Tyler Burge's *Origins of Objectivity* (2010) provides the canonical mid-level case: perception is *objective* (it represents the distal world via constancies) without being *conceptual*. SIG can be cashed out as minimal objectivity-bearing registering, with sub-perceptual registering (chemoreceptor) at lower levels and conceptual articulation at higher levels.

The formal boundary: **SIG registers. SIG does not justify.** Treating SIG as ontologically primitive does not commit EO to foundationalism, because SIG does not function as evidence in the space of reasons. It functions as a register-event in a system whose downstream interpretive operators may or may not be conceptually structured.

The crucial structural feature is **bādha** (sublation): retroactive annulment. When a lower-level appearance is corrected by a higher-level one, the correction is not merely replacement but retroactive negation — "there was never a snake there." SIG-events admit non-monotonic update, where the content of update is total ontological retraction.

The distinction between error and anesthesia is consequential. Error is SIG firing on the wrong content — the rope appearing as a snake. Anesthesia is SIG failing to fire at all — the molecule present but not registering, the signal present but not detected, the pattern visible but not noticed. Both are failure modes, but they have different downstream consequences and different repair operations.

## Helix position and dependencies

SIG presupposes NUL and is presupposed by INS. The dependency is structural.

An appearance cannot register (SIG) without the prior capacity for non-appearance (NUL). The system must be capable of *not* registering — of having a receptor that can respond and doesn't — for a registration to carry information. Without NUL, every receptor fires on everything, which is indistinguishable from no receptors at all. NUL is the ground condition for selective appearing.

Downstream, INS without SIG produces entities without appearing. You can create a record, but without SIG, the record has not shown up for any system — it exists but has not appeared, which means nothing downstream can respond to it. INS needs SIG the way a birth needs a world: not as an afterthought, but as the condition that makes the particular *available* rather than merely existent.

## What SIG does

Three patterns account for most SIG operations in practice. In every case, the transformation is the same: something that was not present-for-the-system becomes present-for-the-system.

### The first appearing

A datum arrives at a junction and registers. Before SIG, undifferentiated sensor readings. After SIG, Wolf-47. The wildlife researcher draws a circle around a cluster of heat signatures, GPS pings, and motion triggers and says: that's a wolf. The circle-drawing is the moment a wolf *appears* in the data. Before it, data points. After it, a persistent identity. The *decision* to circle those readings was SIG — ephemeral attention, the wolf showing up for the researcher. The *creation* of the Wolf-47 entity is INS. The *assignment* of subsequent heat signatures to Wolf-47 is DEF. If two researchers disagree about whether a reading belongs to Wolf-47 or Wolf-52, that's DEF holding conflict as data. If the wildlife service decides Wolf-47 is actually a coyote, that's REC — the frame changed, every observation gets reinterpreted.

### The pointing child

A child in a room full of objects extends a finger. Before the pointing, every object was equally present in the visual field. After the pointing, one object has appeared — it has become present-for-the-dyad of child and caregiver. Nothing about the object changed. Nothing was created. Nothing was classified. Something was registered.

This is SIG at the pre-linguistic level. The pointing is the appearing made visible. The child does not yet have words. The object does not yet have a name. What has happened is simpler: something showed up.

### SIG as ephemeral attention

In data systems, SIG is the operator that does not produce enduring log entries. Clicks, selections, focuses, hovers — these are micro-appearances. The cursor appearing over this element rather than that one. The user's eye landing on this row. The system's attention being directed here rather than there.

The EO///DB manual specifies: "SIG does not carry values. Creating a table is INS. Declaring what it tracks is DEF. The *decision* to create the table was SIG — ephemeral attention in a meeting." SIG is where you are standing in the building, not a wall in the building.

This ephemeral character follows from the appearing reading. Appearance is inherently temporal. It is an event, not a state. "The food arrived." "Someone came." "The flowers showed up." You cannot store an appearance the way you store a created entity. You can only note that it happened.

## The three confusions

English conflates three ontologically distinct operations that EO separates. Under the appearing reading, the separations are cleaner than they were under "distinction":

**SIG (appearing):** Something shows up for a system. The molecule appears to the receptor. The pizza is available. The flowers arrived. Before the operation, the thing was not present-for-the-system. After, it is. The transformation is relational: something-to-a-system.

**SEG (partitioning):** Already-appeared things get organized into structured groups with boundaries between them. The appearing has already happened (SIG has already fired). Now SEG draws walls. SEG is not the appearing — it is the structuring of what has appeared.

**DEF (establishing what holds):** An appearance gets interpreted. The molecule was present (SIG). Now it is labeled "glucose" (DEF). The appearing persists — something is still registered — but meaning has been assigned. This is not a new SIG operation. It is DEF operating on SIG's output. The Advaitic term *adhyāsa* (superimposition) names the error of confusing the interpretation with the appearance.

The practical cost of conflation: if you think you need a new partition (SEG) when what you actually need is a new appearance (SIG), your intervention targets structure when the problem is at the level of existence. And if you think something new has appeared (SIG) when what you actually need is a reinterpretation of an existing appearance (EVA on DEF), you will proliferate entities when you should be shifting significance.

## SIG as composition target

Every operator can take SIG's output as its input.

**NUL(SIG(x)):** Disappearing. Letting an appearance pass without reinforcement. The Korean term is *haeje* — the deliberate dropping of honorifics. Something had appeared; now the system declines to maintain it. The couple who stop introducing each other as "my husband" and "my wife" and let the appearances dissolve — not replaced by new ones, just unreinforced until they pass away. The molecule is still there after the receptor stops firing.

**SEG(SIG(x)):** Partitioning an appearance. Taking what has appeared and drawing boundaries within it. "Revenue" appeared as a single category; now it is partitioned into "Revenue (GAAP)" and "Revenue (Internal)." The appearance persists, but it has been structured.

**CON(SIG(x)):** Connecting an appearance. Linking what showed up to something else. "This customer is also an employee." Two SIG events joined by CON. This is Bateson's second half — the "makes a difference" that connects the registered to the downstream.

**EVA(SIG(x)):** Evaluating an appearance. Same thing registered, different significance. The molecule that appeared as "sugar" is now evaluated as "toxin." The appearance persists — something is still registered — but its status has changed.

**REC(SIG(x)):** Reconstructing the conditions of appearing. The frame within which things show up gets rebuilt. Wolf-47 isn't just reclassified from wolf to coyote (that would be EVA). The entire framework within which "wolf" and "coyote" are distinct categories gets reconstructed. This is what happens when a paradigm shifts: not just the labels change, but what can appear changes.

The population distribution follows the universal figure-bias: SIG × Figure (58 verbs in English) dominates SIG × Ground (11) and SIG × Pattern (27). Humans are fluent at registering specific things — things showing up for us. Korean is the diagnostic outlier. SIG accounts for 27% of Korean's classified verb inventory — triple any other language in the corpus. Korean grammar forces appearing-registration before every utterance. The honorific system, the speech-level system, the topic-marking particles — all require the speaker to register the relational context before a single content word is spoken. Korean is a SIG-saturated language.

SIG accounts for 96 verbs in the English corpus (8.1% of classified verbs), making it the fourth-smallest operator after REC (26), DEF (30), and SYN (31). This moderate size reflects a real cognitive property: registering is neither the rarest human operation nor the most common. We do it constantly — but most of what we call "noticing" is actually a complex operation (naming, classifying, diagnosing) that involves SIG as one component among several.

The German Act face exemplars are the strongest empirical evidence for the appearing reading. The top-classified SIG clauses from German service reviews are almost uniformly reports of presence or availability: "Pizza is available there." "The flowers arrived at exactly the right time." "Someone finally came." "I was amazed at how quickly the food arrived." "Scandinavian fashion brands became available here too." The semantic pattern is consistent: observational present-tense registration of what is or isn't present. These are not distinctions being drawn. They are reports of appearing. The German review corpus may be the cleanest SIG isolate in the dataset precisely because review-writing is a register where people report what registered — minimal structural overlay, minimal interpretive framing.

## The second-pass form: SIG²

After the helix completes its first pass (NUL through REC), each operator encounters itself-plus-the-trace-of-REC. SIG² is not naive appearing but accountable appearing — the capacity to register with full awareness that the registration is shaped by the system's own frame.

First-pass SIG is naive: the receptor fires because the molecule is there. The appearing feels like it was found. Second-pass SIG² is situated: the system has all nine operators available and registers while knowing that what shows up is frame-dependent. The difference is the difference between a diagnosis delivered as fact and a diagnosis delivered as the best available interpretation given the current evidence and framework.

SIG² is what the CLM analysis identified as "situated assertion" — registration that carries the trace of the full helix. The parallel to NUL² (deliberate non-action, the Buddhist *śūnyatā*) is precise: NUL² is the choice to not-act with awareness that action is available; SIG² is the choice to register with awareness that the registration is contingent.

## Crosswalk: theories of appearing

### Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form*

Spencer-Brown's instruction "draw a distinction" produces a marked state and an unmarked state. Under the appearing reading, this instruction is not SIG but a SIG + SEG composite. The "drawing" is a partitioning act (SEG). What Spencer-Brown does not name — and what his calculus assumes as given — is the prior condition: that the unmarked space is *available as a space in which marks can register*. That availability is SIG. The calculus presupposes SIG (a markable field in which marks can appear) and begins with SEG (the first mark, which partitions the field).

### Bateson's information

Bateson defined information as "a difference which makes a difference" (1972). The formulation is a composition: SIG followed by CON. The first "difference" is SIG — something registers, appears, shows up. The second "makes a difference" is CON — the registration connects to something downstream, producing a consequence. Bateson's definition is a two-operator pipeline: SIG → CON. Shannon information foregrounds the registration. Pragmatic information foregrounds the consequence. Both are right about their half.

### Peirce's categories

SIG is best understood as the *Firstness of Secondness* — the qualitative shining-forth in an actual receptive event. Firstness (monadic quality-as-such, prior to any encounter) is the phenomenal substrate. Secondness (brute dyadic encounter) is the event structure. SIG is the qualitative registering that happens when encounter occurs. Full sign-relations (Peirce's Thirdness) are several layers above SIG.

### Heidegger's threefold

Heidegger's *Sein und Zeit* §7 gives a canonical threefold that maps directly to SIG's internal structure:

1. **Phänomen** (self-showing): SIG-proper. What shows itself from itself.
2. **Schein** (semblance): The failure mode. What shows itself *as* what it is not. The rope-snake.
3. **Erscheinung** (indicative appearance): The semiotic mode. What announces itself *through* another. A symptom.

The later Heidegger's *es gibt* formula — Being and time are *given*, and the "it" of the giving is *Ereignis*, the event of mutual appropriation — is the most rigorous Western articulation of an appearing-event prior to subject/object. For SIG: no agent behind the appearing.

### Uexküll's Umwelt

Jakob von Uexküll's Umwelt theory confirms that SIG is system-relative. Each organism lives in its own world of appearances — what shows up for a tick (butyric acid, warmth, hairlessness) is not what shows up for a human. The *Merkmal* (perceptual cue) is what appears; the *Wirkmal* (effector cue) is the downstream response. SIG is the Merkmal side. The tick does not draw a distinction. Three things appear to it; that is its world.

### Derrida's *différance*

*Différance* is not SIG. It is the observation that SIG(SIG(SIG(...))) never terminates — that each registration opens the need for further registrations, and no final ground is reached. In EO terms, this is the recognition that SIG applied recursively produces an infinite regress of meta-registrations. Derrida describes the infinite depth of the SIG column. EO treats each level as a real operation rather than evidence of impossibility.

## What goes wrong without SIG

Without SIG, the system cannot register. Every input arrives undifferentiated. Nothing shows up. Nothing appears. The system is anesthetic.

The consequences cascade through the entire helix. If SIG is degraded, INS has nothing to instantiate — you can create data, but it has not shown up for any system, so nothing downstream can respond to it. SEG has nothing to partition — you can draw boundaries, but without prior appearances, the boundaries separate nothing-registered from nothing-registered. CON has nothing to connect — a join on content that hasn't appeared is a Cartesian product, an unconstrained combinatorial explosion that encodes no structural information.

The specific failure modes are visible in institutional practice. A case management system that cannot register the difference between "client referred for housing" and "client referred for mental health" treats every referral as the same. An intake form that uses a single free-text field instead of typed fields has degraded SIG: every piece of information arrives unregistered, and the clinician must perform SIG manually — noticing what is present in the text that the system refused to notice in its schema.

The deepest failure is not in the data. It is in the system's relationship to its own appearances. A system without SIG does not know what has shown up for it and what hasn't. It cannot tell whether a category was registered or imposed, whether a type reflects what appeared or reflects the schema designer's assumptions. Everything looks the same — present — because the system has no capacity for not-present. And without the not-present as a reference point, the present becomes invisible. You stop noticing what is showing up because you have no capacity for it not showing up.

## Open questions

**The MECE control.** The cross-linguistic study shows that EO's 3×3×3 partition produces strong embedding-space separation (+16.15 composite z-score). The outstanding question is whether any three-part jointly-exhaustive partition would produce comparable separation, or whether EO's specific partition is privileged. The MECE control experiment (five 3×3×3 partitions including EO plus four controls) is the single highest-leverage remaining falsification target.

**SIG × Ground population.** With 11 verbs in English, registering conditions (as opposed to entities or patterns) is a sparse cell. Whether this sparsity reflects a genuine cognitive limitation — humans struggle to register ambient conditions — or a lexicalization gap — the conditions register but unnamed — is an open empirical question.

**SIG² and professional expertise.** Whether SIG² (accountable registration) can be reliably measured — whether it is possible to design an instrument that distinguishes naive SIG from accountable SIG² — is untested.

**The performative dimension.** The Yoruba concept of *àṣẹ* suggests that SIG has a constitutive performative pole: to register is to register-as-effective. Appearing, on the *àṣẹ* model, is not passive but a bringing-to-bear of content on a system's subsequent dynamics. Whether EO should formally encode this performative dimension — and if so, how — is unresolved.

**Verb reclassification under Appearing.** Some verbs currently in the SIG cell are partition-verbs (*classify, categorize, sort, type*) that may belong closer to SEG's territory under the appearing framing, since partitioning is structurally a boundary operation. Other verbs currently in adjacent cells may migrate inward: *notice, detect, sense, register, spot* — verbs of salience and registration. A reclassification under the appearing semantic context should produce tighter clustering in embedding space, cleaner boundaries with SEG and DEF, and better classification accuracy. If it produces worse results, that would be evidence that "Distinction" was the right level of abstraction after all.
